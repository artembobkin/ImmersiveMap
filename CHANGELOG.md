# Changelog

All notable changes to ImmersiveMap will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project aims to follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html)
once the public API stabilizes.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The flat map's horizon wears the globe's air. The horizon fog gains the
  atmosphere's three-part profile turned inward: a dense band hugging the
  horizon, a wide faint glow down the plain and a whitening right at the
  line, in the halo's blue, measured by each pixel's angle below the
  horizon (`HorizonFogUniform`, 6, 20 and 2 degrees), so the far range of a
  tilted view dissolves into haze well before it reaches the line instead of
  meeting a beige wall half a degree from it. Above the line a new flat sky
  pass (`flatSkyFragmentShader`, drawn after the ground under the far-plane
  depth test) continues the same profile upward, the whitened horizon
  deepening to the halo blue, and blends in with the transition during the
  unfurl so the surface switch happens between identical skies. The seam
  fog by distance stays underneath as the guarantee at the coverage edge.
  Transparent space keeps the old clear-colour fog and paints no sky.

- The footprint fade of the flat ground fills, the vector map's stand-in
  for a mipmap: the fill shader measures how much ground a pixel covers
  (the screen derivatives of the ground position along the longer axis, in
  the source tile's units) and, where that footprint exceeds what the fill's
  detail can resolve, blends the fill toward the style's far tone
  (`GroundFootprintFade`, 24 to 96 tile units per pixel). The default style
  sends every land class, WorldCover and OSM alike, to the vegetation base
  (settlements keep a quarter of their distance) and leaves water and ice at
  full contrast, so the far range of a tilted view becomes one calm plain
  with its lakes and rivers instead of a field of flickering sub-pixel
  blotches; the fill outline fades out over the same band, since a fill too
  thin for a pixel would otherwise draw its outline as a hairline. Ground
  styles carry the far colour pair, so prepared tiles are re-baked (prepared
  format 86).

- Fill-outline antialiasing on the flat map, the `fill-antialias`
  construction of Mapbox GL: every ground fill's ring edges are drawn once
  more as one-pixel line primitives in the fill's colour, alpha by the
  fragment's distance to the projected edge, so the staircase the triangle
  rasterizer leaves on a fill edge softens into a fringe that slides
  continuously under camera motion instead of jumping a pixel at a time.
  The lines index the fill's own vertices (no vertex is added; the parser
  keeps the ring edges as a third class segment of the ground bucket, tile
  boundary edges left out), sit at their fill's rank depth under a
  lessEqual test so a fringe shows only over the layers below its fill,
  and draw only for the styles that are opaque that frame. The globe keeps
  its fills as they were. Prepared tiles are re-baked (prepared format 85).

- The loading globe glows instead of showing space: a luminous near-white
  planet body draws between the stars and the tiles (part of the
  atmosphere's rendering), so a slot whose tile has not arrived reads as
  lit planet and the map appears over it slot by slot. The body is an
  opaque coarse sphere at the very back of the rank-depth band: hidden
  surface removal erases it under every painted slot, so a fully loaded
  globe pays about five hundred vertices and zero fragments for it. It
  brightens toward the limb, hides its coarse silhouette under the
  atmosphere's rim glow, fades out over the unfurl's early window like the
  halo, and disappears with the rest of the space decor under
  `transparentSpace()`.

- `.buildingRoofShapes(isEnabled:)` on `ImmersiveMapView` and
  `ImmersiveMapSettings`, backed by `StyleSettings.buildingRoofShapesEnabled`
  (off by default): whether buildings raise shaped roofs (`roof:shape`)
  where the tiles describe one. Off, every building takes a flat lid at its
  full height and the roof attributes are never parsed. The roofs are baked
  into the prepared tiles, so the flag is part of the prepared-cache
  identity and toggling re-parses the tiles like any other style change.
  Measured on the street benchmark, shaped roofs cost nothing visible on
  the GPU (the roof surface replaces the flat lid rather than adding to
  it); the default is off as a stylistic choice, not a performance one.

- Road paint gets a distance LOD: past the camera distance where the
  thinnest marking is under half a pixel, a tile skips its marking draws
  entirely (the road buckets' detail role wholesale, the ground bucket per
  run), which is invisible by construction because the analytic
  antialiasing has already faded that paint to a sub-pixel smear. Label
  placement compute runs every frame again: the every-other-frame reuse
  that briefly accompanied this made labels lag the camera by one frame
  and visibly jitter under a pan, so it is gone.

- The lines classes resolve their style in the fragment stage: the vertex
  exports one flat style index plus the two per-vertex line fields instead
  of the unpacked style, cutting a ribbon vertex's interpolants to a third
  on the sphere and the plane. Receiver shadow sampling (buildings, scene
  models) switched to an exact analytic receiver-plane gradient: no screen
  derivatives, so fragments beyond the shadow fade or fully turned from the
  sun exit before projecting a single cascade. The sphere's ribbon split
  grid is four times as coarse as the fills (prepared format v84), and the
  flat horizon backdrop draws only its opaque fills (its z3 linework is
  sub-pixel under the horizon fog). Device spans: globe z1-6 ladder p50
  3.5 to 3.1 ms, flat overview 2.3 to 2.2 ms.

- The cascade shadow map is cached across frames (`ShadowMapReuseController`):
  the sun is static and buildings do not move, so the caster pass re-runs only
  when the camera travels outside the margined cascade fit, the light or map
  resolution changes, a caster tile the map has not seen arrives, or scene
  models cast; every other frame reuses the rendered atlas through matrices
  re-materialized for the current pan, and the fitted windows stay glued to
  the map content so shadow edges do not move when a refit lands. The ground
  shadow mask got cheaper too: analytic per-cascade plane gradients replace
  screen derivatives (letting pixels above the horizon or beyond the shadow
  fade exit before sampling), the middle and far cascades read one bilinear
  compare tap instead of the 3x3 tent (the near cascade keeps the tent for
  contact shadows), and the mask renders at 0.4 of the drawable size. On the
  street benchmark the whole shadow cost fell from 2.7 to 3.7 ms per frame to
  0.29 to 0.40 ms per frame, measured across holds, a 20 second pan, and idle.

- The flat passes open with a tile-ownership stencil prepass: one vertex-only
  quad per unique source writes the tile-priority stencil before anything
  draws. The world-pass buildings now test those marks instead of carrying
  slot clip distances, so a substitute's buildings are rejected in streets
  and courtyards of finer tiles too, each building source draws once instead
  of once per slot it stands in, and the building vertex stage exports four
  fewer values. The shadow-caster path keeps its clips (the shadow pass has
  no stencil attachment). Device hold measurements on the street scenario
  stayed at parity or slightly better (span p50 5.7 to 5.6 ms on the
  Manhattan hold, 5.8 to 5.5 ms on the Paris hold); the win concentrates on
  loading frames, where a parent no longer redraws its buildings per slot.

- `.fxaa(isEnabled:)` on `ImmersiveMapView` and `ImmersiveMapSettings`: the one-line switch for the FXAA post-processing pass, which stays off by default (the engine renders at one sample per pixel with the ground lines antialiased analytically; FXAA adds the optional smoothing of geometry silhouettes at the price of one fullscreen pass, and with the overlay merged into the world pass it now smooths the labels too). `.postProcessingSettings(_:)` keeps taking the whole value.

### Removed

- The polar caps' baked edge strip is gone: each cap is one constant style colour, the north cap the palette's open ocean and the south its polar ice (`ImmersiveMapBaseColors`), instead of continuing the tiles' rim colour from a texture. The bake rendered the full ground geometry of every pole-row placement (all four pinned cover zooms) into a 4096x1 texture per pole, and its change hash included continuous inputs (camera zoom, the fades), so every frame of a zoom animation re-baked both strips, at a cost dominated by the dense northern coastlines (a captured bake frame spent 14 percent of its GPU time on the northern strip). Deleted with it: both strip textures and their mips, the strip sampler and its uniform, the single-sample flat tile pipeline that existed only for the bake, and the cap fragment's texture sampling with its hand-computed LOD; the cap fragment is now one constant colour times the unfurl fade. The trade is visible where the tiles paint something other than the palette constant at the rim (Arctic sea ice against a water-coloured cap); either pole is one palette constant away.
- The space background is gone: no nebula, no haze band, no procedural sky at all. The starfield layer used to paint a fullscreen background that evaluated five fractal noise fields (about eighty hashes) and rebuilt the globe's pan rotation matrix per pixel of every frame, making the sky the single most expensive part of a whole-planet frame; briefly it was baked into a texture, and now it is simply removed. Space is the world pass's clear color (`SpaceSettings.clearColor`), already blended toward the flat map's color by the transition, and the starfield layer draws only the stars over it, with the pan rotation computed once per frame on the CPU. On the zoom-0 trace the world pass's fragment channel fell from 5.3 to about 1.4 channel-ms per frame against the fullscreen-nebula sky.
- The earth scene, the atmosphere and the globe's placeholder surface are gone, and only the starfield remains around the planet. The sun, the day/night terminator, the night side, the atmosphere halo and the surface glow are no longer drawn, and every calculation behind them left the shaders: the sphere's ground fragments are the bare style colour on the pure sphere and gain only the horizon fog during the unfurl, the deferred surface-lighting pass is deleted, and the sky (the space background and the stars) draws first again with the tile geometry blending over it. The placeholder grid under the tiles is deleted too, so nothing writes surface depth on the sphere any more: a tile that has not arrived leaves its slot unpainted (the sky shows through the hole), and the consumers of that depth (route and model occlusion by the planet, label occlusion at the limb) are knowingly degraded until they get their own answer. Public API removed with the features: `EarthSceneSettings`, `AtmosphereSettings`, `EarthSceneTimeMode`, the `earthScene(isEnabled:)`, `atmosphere(isEnabled:)` and `atmosphereSettings(_:)` modifiers on `ImmersiveMapSettings` and `ImmersiveMapView`, and the Earth scene switch in the debug HUD. The `Examples/macOS/ImmersiveMapSettingsMac` Earth scene section became a Sky section (stars and transparent space).

### Changed

- Fix: the flat map flooded with the horizon backdrop's colours from the layered-ground change. The coarse z3 backdrop still drew FIRST (the old painter's order), and under the rank-depth scheme its written ranks rejected the finer main coverage's background (rank 0, farthest), so the whole city showed the backdrop's overview landcover with only roads, water and translucent layers on top. The backdrop now draws AFTER the main coverage, restoring the sphere's finest-first rule, and the tile-priority stencil carves it out of every pixel the main coverage owns while it still paints to the horizon beyond the coverage's edge. The flat substitute offscreen test now loads the z3 backdrop ancestor on purpose, which reproduces the flood on the old order.
- The flat fills drop the line fields, closing the last gap with the sphere's variants. The flat tile shader used to carry the six analytic line interpolants (and two screen-space derivatives in the fragment) for every draw, fills included; a function constant now splits it exactly like the sphere: the opaque and translucent fill passes neither export, interpolate nor evaluate the line coverage (the colour, with the fade already folded in the vertex stage, is final), while the ribbons, the road buckets and the bridge overlay keep the full line pipeline. The flat drawer also gained the sphere's capture debug groups (ground.opaqueFills / translucentFills / lineRibbons, roads). On the flat9 scenario the world fragment channel fell from 2.03 to 1.44 channel-ms per frame and the GPU frame span p50 from 3.2 to 2.4 ms.
- The flat ground draws as class layers under a depth write, the sphere's scheme. The flat surface used to blend every layer of every tile over every pixel; now its z is the same far-plane rank band the sphere uses (per style, computed in the vertex stage, with a per-group offset placing ground fills, ground ribbons and the road buckets in disjoint bands), the opaque fill layers draw first, unblended, writing the band, so hidden surface removal shades a pixel once by its topmost opaque layer, and the translucent fills and ribbons follow, tested. The band is farther than every real fragment, so the buildings' occlusion of the ground is untouched, and it is the flat opaque pass that owns the tile-priority stencil now. On the flat9 scenario the world fragment channel fell from 4.27 to 2.03 channel-ms per frame (the overview palette turned out mostly alpha-opaque, so the layered pass removes about half the overdraw) and the GPU frame span from p50 5.5 / p90 6.7 / p99 8.8 to 3.2 / 5.0 / 5.7.
- The tile priority lives in the stencil buffer, on both surfaces. The depth attachment became depth32Float_stencil8 (about 1 extra byte per pixel of transient tile memory), and WHICH tile owns a pixel is now one mechanism everywhere: the ground's owner pass writes the source's priority (its zoom, `TileSourceStencilPriority`) into the stencil and every tile pass tests greaterEqual, so a coarser substitute's overflow fails wherever a finer tile painted. On the sphere this replaces the source-zoom depth band (the rank band shrinks to class and style; measured parity on the device). On the flat map it unlocks what depth could not touch there, because the flat depth belongs to the buildings: the ground, road and bridge tiles lost their four slot clip distances, and the flat drawer deduplicates placements to unique (source, world-wrap loop) pairs drawn once at full extent, finest first. Clip distances remain only on the buildings and as the morph's unroll cut. New offscreen test pins the flat substitute rejection (mirror of the sphere's); the new `flat9` bench scenario covers the flat overview zooms. Measured there: world vertex channel 1.34 to 1.12 channel-ms per frame, span p99 9.2 to 8.8 ms; the loading and pan frames gain the most, where a coarse tile standing in several slots used to draw once per slot.
- The flat map takes the sphere's clean wins. The zoom fade folds into the vertex stage on the plane too (Tile.metal multiplies it into the colour's alpha; the fragment neither interpolates the fade mask nor walks the fade bands, for the ground and every road bucket alike), and the flat ground draws by its run table: runs whose fade is exactly 0 this frame (road classes below their start zooms at overview, markings below street zoom) are skipped before a single buffer is bound, and the visible survivors coalesce into contiguous index spans, so the common frame still draws one span per tile. The depth-band substitute rejection stays sphere-only on purpose: the flat depth buffer belongs to the buildings, and a source-zoom bias band would punch through building bases near the ground contact, so flat substitutes keep their slot clip distances.
- The morph draws through the same layered machinery as the resting sphere. The unfurl used to be one combined blended draw per placement behind five clip distances (four slot edges plus the cut); the unrolled surface never self-intersects toward the camera (back-face culling owns what curls away), so the rank depth band applies to it unchanged, and the morph now gets everything the resting sphere got: the opaque-fills depth pass, the translucent and ribbons class passes, zero-fade run skipping, run coalescing, unique-source deduplication with full-extent substitutes rejected by the source-zoom band, and only the unroll's cut left as a clip distance. One code path for both worlds; on the zoom 1-6 trace the morph leg's vertex channel eased from 2.33 to 2.18 channel-ms per frame and the scenario's span p99 from 4.8 to 4.6 ms, with the bigger effect on loading frames mid-morph, where a coarse tile standing in many slots now draws once.
- The space décor is skipped whenever no sky pixel is on screen. At region zooms the planet fills the whole viewport, but the stars still rasterized (drawn first, painted over by the opaque ground) and the atmosphere still ran its analytic profile over every pixel of a fullscreen pass. A frame-level test (four viewport corner rays against the sphere; the planet's projection is convex, so the corners decide the frame, `GlobeSkyVisibilityMath`) now gates both layers on the resting sphere; mid-morph they draw and fade with the transition as before. The skip is exact, so nothing visible changes. Zoom 1-6 trace: world fragment channel 1.89 to 1.37 channel-ms per frame, GPU frame span p50 3.5 / p90 4.8 / p99 5.4 to 3.2 / 4.1 / 4.8.
- The resting sphere's slot clip is gone: the depth band rejects substitutes instead. Every sphere vertex used to compute and export four clip distances so a coarse tile standing in for a missing slot would be cropped to it, and a source standing in for several slots was drawn once per slot, whole each time. The ground's rank depth now orders source zoom first (a per-draw bias in the tile uniform), class second, style third, sources draw finest first, and every tile's opaque background quad writes depth across its whole slot, so a coarser source's overflow simply fails the depth test wherever a finer tile painted; unique sources draw once at full extent. The band's step shrank to 4e-7 so ten zoom bands stay within about 2e-3 of the far plane, well behind the sphere's own limb and the caps, routes and models that depth-test against real geometry (mirrored by GlobeSurfaceDepthRank, pinned with the shader by the clip contract tests; a new offscreen test pins that an exact child rejects its parent substitute inside its slot while the siblings still get covered). The morph keeps its five clips (four slot edges plus the unroll's cut), the flat map keeps its clip path. Zoom 1-6 trace: world vertex channel 2.39 to 1.77 channel-ms per frame, GPU frame span p50 4.0 / p90 5.4 / p99 6.2 to 3.5 / 4.8 / 5.4; the zoom-0 control improved too (vertex 0.67 to 0.48, span p50 1.9 to 1.7).
- Point-locked ribbons are provisioned at 12 tile units per point of visible width instead of 32 (prepared format 83). A tile is at least 512 points across at its native level, so one point is at most 8 tile units; the old ceiling made every border and overview road stroke a quad almost three times wider than its stroke could ever reach, and each of those extra fragments ran the full coverage math to produce alpha 0. On the new zoom 1-6 scenario the world fragment channel fell from 2.14 to 1.74 channel-ms per frame and the GPU frame span from p50 4.2 / p90 6.1 / p99 6.9 to p50 4.0 / p90 5.4 / p99 6.2; the visible stroke is unchanged (the point edge resolves in screen space and only clamps to the rim), with half again of rim headroom kept for the sphere's foreshortening toward the limb.
- A ground run whose zoom fade is exactly 0 this frame is skipped on the CPU instead of rasterized with alpha 0. The sphere drawer asked only "is the fade exactly 1" (to draw opaque); everything else, including whole invisible layers, went to the blended passes and paid full vertex and fragment price for no pixels: at zoom 0 that was nearly every line ribbon on screen (road strokes below their start zoom 5, class-faded boundaries below their start zooms). `TileStyleFadeMath` gained the mirrored `fadeIsZero`, both blended passes filter on it before a buffer is bound, and the fade-band thresholds are pinned by `TileStyleFadeMathTests`. On the zoom-0 trace the world fragment channel fell from 1.56 to 1.12 channel-ms per frame, the vertex channel from 0.94 to 0.67, and the GPU frame span from p50 2.6 to 1.9 ms, matching the no-ribbons experiment almost exactly: the ribbons this scene was paying for were the invisible ones.
- The line ribbons split on a sphere grid twice as coarse as the fills (prepared format 82): a long border extruded to quads pays the subdivision in vertices more than anything else on the sphere, and a thin line tolerates twice the chord sag that would show on a fill edge between two contrasting areas. On the zoom-0 trace the world vertex channel fell from 1.07 to 0.94 channel-ms per frame and the GPU frame span from p50 2.8 / p99 3.3 to p50 2.6 / p99 2.8.
- The sphere vertices take the Gudermannian shortcut: sin and cos of the latitude come straight from the vertex's Mercator row through one exponential (sin lat = tanh x, cos lat = sech x), so the latitude angle is never computed and the atan + sinh chain with the sincos of its result is gone from every ground vertex; the morph also stops round-tripping the flat target through the angle (uv to latitude and back to the same Mercator coordinate through log and tan), taking the vertex's own world uv instead, with the -1..1 north-positive convention and its clamp preserved. Honestly measured at zero on the zoom-0 trace (vertex channel 1.071 to 1.071 channel-ms per frame): the channel is fetch- and export-bound, so the arithmetic hides behind memory waits. Kept because it is strictly less work per vertex, the morph sheds five transcendentals against the resting sphere's three (and the zoom-0 scene never morphs), and the round trip was noise waiting to happen.
- The sphere's ground layers merge their draw calls. The layer rank depth is computed in the vertex stage from the vertex's own style index instead of arriving as a per-draw uniform (the ribbons class sits in its own depth band nearer than every fill, so a ribbon still passes the test over its own style's fill), and the opaque fill layers no longer draw in reversed order: on a TBDR GPU hidden surface removal resolves opaque geometry by depth before shading regardless of submission order, which the zoom-0 trace confirms (world fragment time unchanged to the hundredth). With the rank in the shader, adjacent runs headed for the same pass coalesce into one indexed draw, cutting the frame's ground draw calls to roughly a third; a GPU-side no-op, the saving is command encoding.
- The polar caps' vertex stage takes the frame's composed sphere matrix (`GlobeFrameConstants.sphereClip`, the same path the tile vertices take) instead of rebuilding the pan rotation (an `atan(exp(pi))` and four trigonometric terms) and the translation per vertex. Hygiene, not a measured win: the two fans carry a few thousand vertices. Not a pixel changes; the offscreen cap tests pin both poles and the seam.
- The globe has an atmosphere again, rebuilt lean. One fullscreen draw right after the globe surface and the polar caps resolves, per pixel, how far the view ray passes from the limb and paints the scattered light there: a bright band hugging the limb, a wide faint glow into space, a whitening right at the edge, and, new, a narrow rim glow decaying inward over the surface, continuous across the limb (nothing on the sphere writes depth any more, so the inside is shaped analytically instead of being depth-rejected). Pure arithmetic, no texture reads, and measured at zero within noise on the zoom-0 trace. No sun, no day side and no settings: the look is fixed constants (sky-blue halo, the old designed profile), part of the globe's stock appearance, gated with the starfield so transparent space drops it, and faded out over the first third of the unfurl.
- The line ribbons are back on the resting sphere, and the ground bucket's two classes are now separate index segments baked by the parser instead of a shader-side kill. A prepared tile's ground indices are ordered polygon fills first, line ribbons second, each segment grouped by ascending style with the class recorded in the run table (`GroundStyleRun` gained a class flag, prepared format 81), so the sphere's fill passes never fetch or transform a ribbon vertex and the ribbons pass never touches a fill; the function-constant split that degenerated the other class's primitives with NaN positions is deleted. The paint order changes with the split, everywhere the ground bucket draws (sphere, morph, flat overview, cap bake): every ground ribbon (boundaries, overview strokes) now paints above every ground fill, where before a translucent fill of a higher style could cover a ribbon of a lower one. The sphere subdivision grid also returns to 64x64 on a whole tile (step 64 to 1024 units by zoom): with the ribbons drawing again, the two-pixel chord sag of the coarser grid would bend them visibly against the analytic antialiasing.
- The overlay pass folds into the world pass whenever no scene model is on screen. Labels, avatars and the debug overlay used to always draw in their own render pass, which reloads and re-stores the whole drawable; the pass exists for the scene-model label-occlusion depth prepass, so on frames without drawn models (every whole-planet frame among them) the overlay layers now draw at the end of the world pass, with the label depth test off (there is nothing to test against), and the separate pass with its own cleared depth returns only when models are drawn. With FXAA on, merged labels are antialiased with the rest of the frame, since the post-processing pass now runs last.
- The unroll's cut follows the journey, not a fixed cap. Content past a pole or past the point opposite the view has a Mercator home half a map sideways, and its chart line lawfully crosses the visible region (from a Moscow view, the Arctic beyond the pole swept across the sky mid-morph): no pointwise morph into Mercator can avoid that travel, it can only hide it. A vertex is now clipped while its remaining chart travel exceeds a quarter of the planet radius and appears only when it is nearly home: at transition 0 the hidden set is exactly the content whose two charts disagree, never visible on the resting sphere, and by 1 everything has arrived, so the cut opens and closes without a pop. Markers, routes and scene models gate their mid-morph visibility on the same clearance, so nothing flies across the view on its way to its flat position. The numeric sweep now pins both harms among unclipped content near the view: no folds, and no long-haul travellers.
- The unroll travels straight chart lines and carries its own cut. The morph no longer blends a point's surface distance and bearing separately (the bearing blend snapped through its degenerate zone and stretched far-side triangles into slivers across the view): a point's two flat images, the azimuthal-equidistant projection about the view centre and the Mercator position, are blended as plane points, and the blended chart point wraps onto the growing sphere; endpoints stay exact. A sphere cannot unroll without a cut, and the cut is dynamic, the cap more than 150 degrees of arc from the view centre: inside it the two charts comb the polar fan apart and the blend genuinely folds, so the morph vertex stage clips that cap as a fifth clip distance (one constant, no camera dependence). A numeric sweep test (GlobeUnrollFoldTests) pins that every residual fold of the blend is harmless: inside the clipped cut, or painting at least half a planet radius from the view centre, far off-screen at the zooms the morph runs at.
- The sphere's ground draws as layers under a depth test instead of blending every layer over every pixel. The parser's ground bucket is already one contiguous index run per style in paint order; the prepared tile now carries that run table (`GroundStyleRun`, prepared format 80), and the resting-sphere drawer classifies each run per frame (palette alphas 1 and zoom fade exactly 1 means opaque) and draws the opaque runs top layer first, unblended, writing a per-layer rank depth in a narrow band at the far plane, so the depth test rejects every fragment a higher opaque layer already painted and a pixel is shaded once by its topmost opaque layer. The translucent runs follow bottom-to-top with the same rank depth, tested without writing, so an opaque layer above them still hides them and everything else blends as before; the morph keeps the single blended draw. Placements are clipped to disjoint slots, so the rank is per tile.
- The polar caps cull back faces. Both cap fans now carry the same counter-clockwise winding seen from outside the sphere (the generator's triangle order, pinned by an offscreen render test per pole) and draw with back-face culling, the way the tile mesh does; culling used to be disabled for them, and the far half of each polar fan was kept invisible only by the surface depth the deleted placeholder grid wrote, so it had started to draw through the planet as a pale disc.
- The sphere-to-plane morph is an unroll, and the sphere occlusion clip is gone everywhere. The surface now lives on a sphere of radius `R / (1 - t)` tangent to the view centre: every point keeps its distance along the surface from the view centre (blended from the great-circle arc toward the flat Mercator distance) and its azimuth, and the growing sphere flattens the cap into the plane, exactly the sphere at t = 0 and exactly the Mercator world at t = 1. The surface stays a single-valued convex cap throughout, so nothing ever morphs through the planet's interior, and the per-vertex sphere-as-occluder test (with its square roots and its fifth clip distance) is deleted: back-face culling alone removes the far side. The unfurl wave (the per-point lag phase) is gone with it, along with the marker unfurl-gate machinery it required; the route clearance now tests the growing sphere itself and needs no per-vertex release. The tile sphere shader is split in two: `tileSpherePureVertexShader` draws the resting sphere with no morph code at all (one CPU-composed matrix takes the unit earth direction straight to clip space), and `tileSphereMorphVertexShader` alone carries the unroll and the horizon fog; shared pieces live in headers (`GlobeUnroll.h`, `GlobeTileProjection.h`, `TileShading.h`), and the CPU mirrors (`GlobeUnrollMath`, `GeoScreenProjectionMath`, `GeoSurfaceFrameMath`, the label placement kernel) follow the same construction term for term. `GlobeFrameConstants` carries everything a frame can precompute: the composed sphere-to-clip and sphere-to-world matrices and the unroll curvature.
- The globe frame sheds four costs at once, aimed at the whole-planet zooms. The deep-space tone (`globeSurfaceDeepen`, the desaturate-and-deepen curve below zoom 2) is removed: the planet now wears the tile palette at every zoom, and with it gone the deferred-lighting gate simplifies to "the pure sphere defers", so every transition-0 frame blends its ground layers unlit and the lit-inline shading (with its per-vertex normals and four varyings) exists only for the unfurl; the dead lit-pure pipeline variants are deleted. The placeholder grid under a slot a tile placement already paints draws depth-only with no fragment stage at all (the tile background covers every pixel of the slot anyway); the colour fill remains for uncovered slots, and at the very limb the tile chords can now show the pass clear colour for a pixel or two where they sag inside the grid's silhouette. The per-draw sphere uniforms carry the tile's uv origin, scale and unwrap centre precomputed, so the vertex stages do no per-vertex `pow(2, z)` and the grid slot no longer derives its Mercator row bounds per vertex. And the render sample count is 1 everywhere instead of 4: the ground's lines are antialiased analytically in the tile shaders, and the 4x world pass cost outweighed the edge smoothing it bought on geometry silhouettes (building edges on the flat map are the visible trade); the resolve machinery stays, one constant away.
- The sphere subdivision grid is one step coarser: a whole tile is 32x32 cells instead of 64x64 (step 128 to 2048 tile units by zoom), so every ground polygon drawn on the globe carries a quarter of the split vertices, and prepared tiles up to z9 shrink accordingly on disk and in GPU memory (prepared tile format 79). The trade is chord sag: about two pixels per cell at a tile's native zoom instead of half a pixel, still under the width of the thinnest drawn line. Honestly measured on the zoom-0 bench scene: the world vertex channel did not fall in wall milliseconds at a quarter of the vertices (the channel is fetch- and export-bound and the GPU clock scales down at light loads, inflating per-operation time), so the win is the geometry itself: parse time, tile bytes and vertex fetches, not a channel-ms headline.

- On the pure sphere the ground bucket draws as two isolated class passes, the fills first and the line ribbons second, each a function-constant specialization of the sphere pipeline that clips the other class's primitives whole; the morph and the flat surface keep the single combined draw. The class is a property of the geometry, not the style (a fill shares its style with its decoration, so the style's edge threshold marks both): ribbons carry a non-zero per-vertex side distance, fills carry zero, and no triangle mixes the two. The picture matches the combined draw, and the split is the scaffold the globe performance work isolates the classes with, either pass a one-line toggle in `GlobeVectorSurfaceDrawer`. A manual, opt-in diagnostic joins the tests (`RealTileEndpointDiagnosticTests`, gated behind `IMMERSIVEMAP_REAL_TILES_DIAG=1`): it renders the live endpoint offscreen at the sphere and mid-morph and asserts the centre of the frame is painted, catching regressions that only real content shows; without the variable it skips, so the suite still never touches the network.
- The globe vertex stages take their per-frame constants precomputed and fold the morph away on the pure sphere. Every ground vertex on the sphere used to rebuild the pan rotation matrix, the flat morph target's map size and its Mercator pan (all pure functions of the frame's globe uniform), then compute the flat target, the unfurl phase and the mix even at transition 0, where they contribute nothing; the values now arrive in a `GlobeFrameConstants` uniform computed once per frame, and a pure-sphere function constant removes the dead morph math whenever the frame sits at transition 0, one rule (`GlobeSphereVertexPath`) consulted by the placeholder fill and the tile geometry alike. Honestly measured on the benchmark tour: within run noise (the vertex channel of a globe frame moved 1.88 to 1.83 channel-ms against a spread of about 0.15), consistent with the channel being fetch- and export-bound rather than ALU-bound; kept because it is strictly less work per vertex and the pure-sphere fold is the shape later geometry work builds on.
- The globe surface is lit once per pixel instead of once per ground layer. On the pure sphere at the plain palette, everything `globeSurfaceShade` does to a colour is affine: the day/night factor multiplies, the rim light and the limb glow add, and the horizon fog's strength is the transition and so zero, which means the lighting commutes exactly with alpha blending. The ground layers (the placeholder fill and the tile geometry on the sphere) therefore blend bare style colours, and a new `globeSurfaceLighting` draw right after them resolves each pixel's place on the sphere from the view ray, the way the atmosphere halo does, and hands the light to the fixed-function blend: the additive part in rgb, the multiplier in alpha, applied per sample, with a `greater` depth test at the far plane keeping space untouched for the sky. The moment the lighting stops being affine (the unfurl brings the fog and the morphed silhouette, the deep-space tone wakes below zoom 2) every layer lights itself inline as before; one rule, `GlobeSurfaceLightingPath`, is consulted by the fill, the tiles and the lighting pass alike, so they can never disagree within a frame. Not a pixel changes. On the benchmark tour a globe frame's fragment time went from 4.32 to 4.15 ms and the GPU frame span's p99 from 13.6 to 13.3; the win is small because the globe's effective overdraw turned out low, and its real value is that the ground layers' fragment work is now bare of lighting. The unlit variants also shed the lighting varyings themselves: the surface position, the two normals and the transition exist in the vertex outputs only when the fragment lights inline (a function constant gates the struct members), so the unlit layers neither export, interpolate nor load them; on the tour that sits within run noise, consistent with the interpolation never having been the bottleneck.
- The globe got cheaper on the GPU without changing a pixel. The sky (the space background, the stars, the Sun and the atmosphere halo) used to be painted first across the whole screen and then mostly painted over by the sphere; it now draws after the globe surface, rasterized at the far plane and depth-tested against the surface depth the placeholder grid wrote, so every pixel the sphere covers rejects a sky fragment before it shades. The polar caps keep drawing after the sky: the poles lie outside the Mercator slots, no grid depth covers them, and the caps must paint over the sky there the way they always did. On an iPhone 15 Pro Max flying the benchmark tour, the world pass on the globe legs went from 834 to 568 channel-ms of GPU time per second (fragment work from 742 to 481), the GPU frame span from 6.3/10.8/17.6 ms to 5.7/8.1/13.8 at p50/p90/p99, and a moving frame from 7.2 to 6.4 channel-ms of GPU time overall; the street-level legs, which draw no sky, stayed put.
- A building vertex is 12 bytes instead of 48: positions are quantized to quarter tile units (14.2 fixed point, under 15 cm at the street-zoom tiles buildings ship at), normals to 8 bits renormalized in the shader, and the per-vertex surface id is gone, since no shader ever read it. Building geometry was the single largest slice of a dense city tile's arena (about a third of its bytes), so those tiles shrink by roughly a quarter in GPU memory and on disk. Prepared tile format 78.
- Two memory defaults came down. The shadow map renders at 1024 pixels per cascade instead of 2048: the resident texture array drops from 25 to 6 MB, the coverage math is untouched, and only texel density changes; the old sharpness is one `.shadows(mapResolution:)` away. The raw tile cache keeps 16 MB in RAM instead of 32: raw bytes are read once per download and the prepared disk cache answers revisits, so the larger in-memory copy bought nothing.
- Tiles stay in GPU memory only while a frame draws them, and the prepared disk cache answers revisits without the network. The in-memory tile cache, a 256 MiB LRU of GPU-ready tiles that offered its idle tail to the OS as purgeable memory, is replaced by a working set: the store holds exactly the tiles the current frame demands (the visible tiles, their fallback parents, the horizon backdrop and the shadow casters) plus the z0-3 world cover, and releases everything else the moment demand stops naming it, so a long flight no longer accumulates hundreds of megabytes of tiles it will not draw again, and a memory warning now drops only the off-screen world cover. The tile loader grew a disk stage in front of the network with its own concurrency: a prepared tile found on disk is shown and the load ends there, freshness coming from the cache's time to live (7 days) and its identity namespace instead of a per-load ETag revalidation, and only a miss downloads. The prepared disk cache is therefore the layer a revisited place comes back from: its default quota grows from 256 MiB to 2 GiB, sized in one line with the new `.preparedTileDiskCacheSize(bytes:)` modifier or its `ImmersiveMapSettings` twin. The debug HUD's tile status gains a disk row, the tile trace gains `tile_disk_lookup_start`, `tile_disk_hit` and `tile_disk_miss`, replaces the `tile_memory_cache_*` events with `tile_store_lookup`, `tile_store_insert`, `tile_store_release` and `tile_store_remove_all` (reporting resident tiles and bytes instead of a cost against a limit), and a `tileDiskRead` signpost joins the tile intervals. The Diagnostics section of `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac` trades its GPU cache slider for a prepared disk cache one, and the camera tour example and the four post apps no longer raise a memory cache to survive a lap.
- The synthetic background under every tile's features is one quad that the ground subdivider cuts on its per-zoom grid, instead of a mesh of 64x64 cells built into every tile at every zoom. On the sphere nothing changes at z0 and z1, where the grid is that fine anyway; from z2 the background carries four times fewer triangles per pair of zooms, and from z10, where the surface is flat, it is two triangles instead of 8192, which is what every tile on screen at street zoom was spending on a flat plane. The subdivider itself now sweeps each grid line through a triangle once, handing the piece below the line to the output, instead of re-cutting every piece it had produced so far by every line: the same triangles, and a tile-spanning quad at z0 takes 22 ms to cut instead of 190. Prepared tile format 77.
- The globe's tile geometry sits on the sphere itself: it no longer depth-tests against the placeholder grid and is no longer lifted off the surface to pass that test. The far side of the planet, which the horizon clip lets through while the surface unfurls, is removed by back-face culling instead: every triangle the parser emits (the ground, the bridge overlay and every road bucket) is now wound counter-clockwise in render space, and the tile drawers on the sphere and on the plane draw only front faces. A debug build asserts the winding at the parser's one funnel, and `TileGeometryWindingTests` pins it through the whole parser. Prepared-cache format 76. While the sphere unfurls into the plane, the far side of the planet morphs along chords through the planet's interior and turns front-facing before it is out, which culling cannot tell; the surface now clips against the sphere itself (every vertex tests whether the planet stands between it and the camera, the way routes already did per fragment), so nothing from the far side paints over the near ground mid-morph. The flat morph target of a tile now unwraps around the tile's own centre instead of wrapping every vertex at the antimeridian on its own, so a coarse far tile whose edge lies on the seam opposite the view centre no longer tears into a band across the whole map while it unfurls.
- The globe draws its tiles as geometry on the sphere. Until now the spherical presentation rasterized every visible tile into pages of a 4096x4096 texture atlas (about 85 MiB each) and draped the atlas over a surface grid, and because the bake keyed on zoom-continuous inputs (the fade alphas, the palette handover, the line width taper) every zoom change in globe view re-baked every page: those re-bakes were the slowest frames of a flight (40 to 70 ms of GPU time at the apex of a city-to-city hop), and the atlas blurred the surface wherever a slot was smaller than the tile's footprint on screen. The tile geometry is now projected onto the sphere in the vertex shader through the same surface morph the grid uses, lit by the same globe shading, and clipped at the horizon geometrically (`GlobeVectorSurfaceRenderSubsystem`, `TileSphere.metal`); every line is drawn at the screen's own density with the analytic antialiasing plus the world pass MSAA, and nothing is re-baked when the zoom moves. On an iPhone 15 Pro Max flying the benchmark tour the GPU frame's p90 went from 19 to 11 ms and its p99 from 45 to 18 ms, the process peak memory from about 950 to 480 MB, and the tour from 78 to 88 frames per second, at a median frame half a millisecond longer (every surface fragment is now shaded rather than sampled). To follow the curvature, the parser splits the ground geometry of tiles up to z9 on a grid of 64 to 1024 units by zoom (a tile-spanning triangle drawn on the globe is a chord), which is a prepared-cache format change (v75). The raster atlas and its bake are gone, along with the Atlas tab of the debug HUD, which listed its pages. The polar caps keep continuing the tiles' colour over the pole: their rim colour now comes from an edge strip per pole, one texel high and a turn of longitude wide, baked from the pole rows' ground geometry whenever the placements or the ground fades change, and the pole fades into that strip's windowed mean as it did with the atlas edge row.

- The flat world pass got cheaper on the GPU, most of all with shadows on over a dense tilted city, where the frame used to take 13 ms of GPU time at the median on an iPhone 15 Pro Max and now takes about 6. Three changes, none of which alters what is drawn. The clip that keeps a retained parent tile inside the slot of the child it stands in for moved from a fragment discard into vertex-stage clip distances, so the rasterizer cuts the geometry and the ground and building shaders never discard; a discard makes the GPU shade every fragment before it knows whether it exists, which had switched hidden surface removal off for the whole world pass. Solid buildings now draw before the ground, and the ground depth-tests (without writing) against them, so everything under a building fails the test unshaded instead of being drawn and painted over. And the shadow of the ground plane is evaluated once per pixel by a small pass right after the shadow map (`groundShadowMask`, an 8-bit screen-sized texture), at half the drawable's resolution, which every blended ground layer reads with one bilinear tap; the landuse fill, the roads in their several roles and the overlays used to sample the three shadow cascades again each for the same pixel. Buildings and scene models keep sampling the cascades per fragment, since their surfaces are not the plane. The shadow-caster pass lost its fragment stage altogether (the clip rides on the same clip distances). The globe path is untouched.

### Deprecated

- `ImmersiveMapSettings.TileSettings.CacheSettings.memoryCacheSizeInBytes` no longer does anything: the in-memory tile store has no byte budget. The property, the `memoryCacheSizeInBytes:` parameter of both `CacheSettings` initializers and of the `tileSettings(...)` overloads on `ImmersiveMapSettings` and `ImmersiveMapView` all still compile and still round-trip the value, but the value is ignored, and two settings that differ only in it now compare equal, so changing it no longer rebuilds the renderer. The property carries a deprecation warning naming `preparedDiskCacheSizeInBytes` and `.preparedTileDiskCacheSize(bytes:)` as the size worth setting.

## [0.7.1] - 2026-08-27

### Changed

- The visible sun no longer paints a sunset across space. The wide orange glow around the disc (a Gaussian a quarter of the screen wide, in a colour that only scattering in air produces) is gone: the sun is a warm disc, white at the centre going gold through the disc and amber in a short bloom about one and a half disc radii wide, and the sky around it stays black. The drama of a sun behind the planet moved into the atmosphere halo, which is now the one source of colour for the air on the limb: where the sun grazes the air (the point a sun behind the planet is about to break through, the whole rim with the sun straight behind, and the two points where the terminator meets the limb otherwise) the halo turns warm and brighter, amber in its body and white-gold at the limb, and the blue on either side and that sunrise band are one ramp along the limb with no seam. The sun pass paints no ring of its own any more (its old warm rim, and the blue-and-gold clash where it overlapped the halo, are gone); at the focus it adds a flare stretched along the limb (the diamond ring) that sits on the already-warm halo, and the surface's last sliver of day along that limb carries a warm rim of its own. The `SunSettings` fields keep their names; `limbHaloIntensity` and `limbHaloWidth` now size that flare, and the defaults moved: `glowIntensity` 0.75 to 0.5 (the bloom is tight now), `limbHaloIntensity` 0.35 to 0.9, `limbHaloWidth` 0.10 to 0.05. New visual review scenarios `sun.behind.limb` and `sun.straight.behind`.

- The globe wears deeper colours when the whole planet is on screen. Up to zoom 1 the surface is drawn slightly muted and with darker midtones than the tile palette (which is a flat cartographic one, deliberately pale so labels read over it up close), and the lit disc falls off toward the limb with the view angle before the atmosphere rim brightens it, so the sphere against black looks like a planet seen through its air rather than an atlas wrapped on a ball; the deepening holds up to zoom 1 and eases back smoothly between zoom 1 and 2, alongside the terminator fade, so it is gone before the sphere starts to unfurl. White stays white and black stays black, so ice caps and coastlines keep their contrast. The tiled surface, the placeholder fill under a still-loading tile and the polar caps deepen together, so no part of the sphere shows in the paler palette next to the rest. New visual review scenario `globe.whole.planet.deep`.

### Fixed

- The polar caps fade into what the tiles show at their rim instead of into a palette colour. What the last tile row holds at 85° changes with zoom (the low-zoom land cover paints the Arctic sea ice white, the detailed layers paint open water), so the northern cap, filled with the palette's water, showed as a dark disc inside the ice at one zoom and, filled with ice, as a white blur on the water at another. The pole colour is now the mean of the edge row of the tile each cap wedge continues, sampled on the GPU, and it is sampled at the mip level the surface reads the rim at, so the cap continues the tiles it sits on in the same shade at every zoom (read from level 0 it stood out as a crisper disc at a far zoom); the palette's `polarIce` (which the northern cap now shares with the southern one) is only what a cap with no tile under it yet draws.

## [0.7.0] - 2026-08-26

### Added

- The built-in style renders a measured streetscape where the tiles carry one. Tiles built with road-graph geometry (osm2streets in the ImmersiveMap tile pipeline) can ship, in the `transportation` layer, the carriageway of every stretch of street as a polygon (`subclass=carriageway_area`, z15+) next to the junction polygons that already shipped, and the road paint as its own line features (`marking` = `dividing`, `lane_separator`, `edge`, `crossing_marked`, `crossing_unmarked`, with `paint` white or yellow and `style` solid or dashed, z15+). A shipped surface draws as fill and kerb exactly like a junction area, clips the ribbons of the streets it covers, and being measured it also ends their synthesized lane paint, which the shipped marking lines replace: dash patterns are lengths in metres locked to the ground (a one-metre lane separator, a two-metre centre line), the stroke stays point-locked with solid lines drawn visibly thicker than broken ones, a measured crossing line stripes itself through the existing zebra builder and silences the attribute-tagged copy of the same crossing in that tile. The `edge` lines travel but draw nothing, and the grey kerb went with them: the automobile tier now draws kerbless at every zoom, the roadway held by its fill against the ground and by the paint on it, the way the lane-level modes of commercial engines draw carriageways. Parking lots keep their kerb (a lot boundary, not a road edge), tunnels and paths never had one, and the casing machinery stays behind a style switch for a palette that wants it back. Everything is data-driven and additive: a tile without the new fields renders exactly as before, so planet coverage without road-graph data is untouched. Surface-versus-ribbon clipping is now also structure-aware, so a bridge deck polygon no longer clips the street running under it on the ground. New visual review scenario `roads.osm2streets.tverskaya` holds the look on the Tverskaya / Mokhovaya / Okhotny Ryad junction. Hand-mapped `area:highway` polygons are no longer drawn where they ship as `junction_area` without `origin=graph`: in central Moscow such an area often covers a whole street including the gap between the two one-way halves of a dual carriageway, welding the reconstructed carriageway bodies into one mass with both inner edge lines stranded inside it; the roadway is filled by the reconstruction alone, and the tag still travels so the decision can be reversed per region. The slits that uncovering left visible are paved by the engine: the road graph trims one street into pieces at every node, and where two pieces simply continue into each other (a joint, not a junction, so no polygon ships for it) a narrow slit stayed open between the two carriageway polygons, showing the ground, the kerbs of both trimmed ends, and the street's fallback ribbon at its own width. The parser now finds each slit by the street's own line and spans the two facing trim edges with a quad drawn exactly like the surfaces it joins, clipping the ribbon out of the slit with them; a slit is paved only between two pieces of the same street id, so the median of a dual carriageway, whose halves are different roads, stays open. Pedestrian paths wear a warm sand tone a clear step under the ground, the way Apple draws park walks: they used to be the exact ground off-white and were invisible until they crossed a park. Road paint stays off the screen until camera zoom 15 and fades in just past it (the band used to open at 14): below street scale the dashes were sub-point noise over the network view. The marking paint became fully opaque: it was slightly translucent, which washed it out against the surface and stamped a denser patch wherever two decoration quads of one colour overlapped; muting now lives in the tone alone. The bus-lane letter A is built with a mitered apex (the legs share one sharp edge instead of two crossing butt caps), a flat baseline and a crossbar running centreline to centreline, and the bus-stop sawtooth is one seamless mitered band with its teeth distributed evenly along the axis, so its joints neither notch nor double-stamp at any corner angle. Surface parking lots joined the same machinery: tiles built with the ImmersiveMap pipeline ship `amenity=parking` polygons as `subclass=parking_area` (z15+, with the mapped `orientation` where OSM has one), and the engine draws them as service-tier asphalt with a kerb plus a synthesized comb of parking-bay stripes laid out the way real lots are painted: perpendicular bays every 2.6 m across the long axis, deep lots alternating a bay row with a driving aisle, parallel parking spread to car length, every stripe clipped to the polygon. The comb is a stylization of the lot, not a claim about individual mapped spaces, which OSM almost never carries. Dedicated bus lanes became visible the same way: the tiles ship each strip of Bus lanes (a contraflow lane derived from `oneway:bus=no`, or a bus way mapped as its own line) as its axis line (`marking=bus_lane`, oriented with the direction of travel), and the engine stamps the letter A along it in the marking paint, feet toward the oncoming driver, the way real asphalt marks a dedicated lane, with a solid separator where the bus lane borders a driving lane of the same road, and every `highway=bus_stop` gets the yellow sawtooth of the stop marking folded along the kerb edge beside it; carriageway polygons now span only the travel lanes, so street-side parking bays in a road's cross-section no longer widen the roadway onto the parking lots beside it, and the bay comb ends at the edge of any overlapping road surface. New visual review scenario `roads.parking.okhotny`.

- Debug HUD: a **Tile grid** overlay with a density picker, in the Controls tab next to Tile layers. It divides the tile under the camera centre, and only that one tile, into a grid of 2, 4, 6 or 8 cells a side and stamps each cell with the tile it belongs to, the cell code and the cell's tile-local bounds, so a screenshot cropped to a single cell still names the slice of tile geometry to go and read. Bounds are the raw MVT units, 0 to 4096 with y growing south from the north edge, so a stamped range pastes into a tile decoder or a grep over tile bodies unchanged; the cell code spells the same box, letter for the column from the west and number for the row from the north. Each stamp sits on a translucent dark plate laid in the tile plane, so a road name or a POI label under a cell cannot read through the meta information; the plate hugs its text rather than filling the cell. While the slot is filled by a coarser substitute tile the stamp gains a fifth `src` line naming the tile the pixels were actually built from, and cells too small on screen to hold the stamp keep their lines and drop their text. The debug polygon pipeline these overlays draw through now blends, which it never did: the alphas already written into the tile outline (0.95) and the label collision boxes (0.9) had been silently discarded and now mean what they say. Like the other map overlays in that tab it is a development-build aid and draws only when the package is compiled in Debug. Documented in `Documentation/docs/performance-and-debug.md`.

- `ImmersiveMapFeatureStyle.pointLockedLine(color:widthPoints:dashLengthPoints:dashGapPoints:)` lets a custom vector-tile style opt into the point-locked line mode the built-in style draws country borders and the overview road skeleton with: the width (and optional dash pattern) is stated in on-screen points and held there at every zoom, the stroke is opaque from the first frame it is visible, ends in butt caps with plain joins, and the tessellated ribbon is provisioned to actually host the stated width. It is the right mode for symbolic lines (borders, networks over a country view) whose weight is a design decision rather than a width on the ground; `.line(color:width:)` stays the world-locked mode whose width lives in tile units. Areal geometry under the mode draws outlines only. Internally the principle now lives in one factory (`FeatureStyle.pointLockedLine`) that the built-in borders and overview roads also draw through, replacing two hand-rolled copies; the built-in rendering is unchanged. The `ImmersiveMapCustomTilesMac` example adopts the mode for its boundary layer.

- `.tileMaximumZoomLevel(_:)` states how deep a tile source goes, next to its `.tileURLTemplate(_:headers:)`. The renderer requests tiles down to that level and scales the deepest level up when the camera zooms past it. The default depth is now z16 (`ImmersiveMapTilesService.maximumTileZoomLevel`, previously 14), matching the tile builds that carry the sub-metre building detail z14 quantization cannot represent; a source that stops shallower states it with the modifier (`.tileMaximumZoomLevel(14)`) so the renderer never asks for tiles the endpoint cannot answer. The field behind it, `tiles.coverage.maximumZoomLevel`, was always public; the modifier makes it a one-liner. Offline region downloads clamp to the same level as before, and the Diagnostics section of `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac` now lets its "Tile zoom cap" slider go to 16.

- A two-finger vertical drag tilts the camera on iOS, the touch counterpart of the macOS right-button drag: fingers side by side moving down tilt further, up levels off, and a full-height drag sweeps the whole pitch range. The drag commits to tilting only once the movement is clearly vertical, and otherwise stays out of the way of the pinch and rotation recognizers sharing the same two touches, so zooming and rotating feel exactly as before. How fast a tilt drag tilts, and which way, is the new `CameraSettings.tiltGestureSensitivity`: the multiple of the pitch range a full-height drag sweeps, shared with the macOS tilt drag (right button, or Option-drag), with the sign picking the direction. The default is 2, so dragging down tilts further and half the view height covers the whole range; a negative value tilts on the way up instead, and the macOS drag used to behave as -1, which restores that feel exactly. The `.tiltGestureSensitivity(_:)` view modifier writes it without constructing a whole `CameraSettings` (zero disables the drag's effect entirely), and the Camera section of `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac` has it on a slider.

- The globe has an atmosphere. A soft halo of scattered light now surrounds the planet's edge, painted in space just outside the sphere, with a matching glow on the surface toward the limb, so the globe reads as a planet with air on it rather than a textured ball with a hard edge against the stars. The halo is resolved per pixel from the view ray and the sphere (not from a circle projected on screen, which detaches from the limb under perspective on a tilted or off-center globe), whitens right at the edge where the scattering saturates, follows the earth scene's sun (full on the day side of the limb, a residual glow on the night side, an asymmetry that fades out with the terminator between zoom 1 and 2), and fades out over the first third of the globe-to-flat morph, before the silhouette it is fitted to starts moving. `ImmersiveMapSettings.AtmosphereSettings` (`isEnabled`, `color`, `intensity`, `thickness`, `sunInfluence`) lives on `SceneSettings.atmosphere`, attached with `.atmosphereSettings(_:)` or the `.atmosphere(isEnabled:)` shorthand, on by default and applied live. Transparent space drops the halo along with the starfield, since it too is painted in space; the surface glow stays. The polar caps and the placeholder fill under still-loading tiles share the surface glow, so nothing seams at the pole or under a loading tile, and the earlier fixed blue limb glow that the surface always carried is replaced by this configurable one (off means a bare sphere). New render layer `atmosphere` between the starfield and the globe surface, drawn by `AtmosphereRenderSubsystem`; three visual review scenarios (`globe.atmosphere.off`, `globe.atmosphere.tilted`, and the updated `globe.default`) hold the look, and the **Earth scene** section of `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac` exposes every field.

- Shadows have a tint. `ShadowSettings.tint` is the RGB cast of a fully shadowed surface on top of the strength darkening, taken on in proportion by partially shadowed fragments; the ground, the extruded buildings and the scene models all apply it, so there is one shadow color across the scene. The default is a cool `(0.88, 0.92, 1.0)`, the bluish cast of light arriving only from the sky, and white restores the neutral darkening. The `Shadow` uniform grew a `tint` field (`ShadowUniformLayoutTests` pins the new layout); the **Buildings and shadows** section of `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac` has a picker for it.

- A rejected tile credential explains itself. A tile request answered with HTTP 401 or 403 now logs one clear warning (os.Logger, subsystem `ImmersiveMap`, outside DEBUG too) instead of only a per-tile debug print: it names the host, and when the request went to the hosted service it says to check the token at https://immersivemap.dev/account, while any other endpoint gets the generic pointer at the two places a credential can travel (the template's query, the `tileURLTemplate(_:headers:)` headers). A server-provided `message` body rides along, and the warning is throttled to one line per minute per process, on its own clock next to the rate-limit notice, so a viewport of refused tiles is one line, not thirty. Offline region downloads share the same download path and the same warning, and the offline example's blocked row now says to check the API key.

- The attribution badge sits tightly in its corner by default, and the distance is configurable: `AttributionSettings.margin` states the gap in points between the badge and the view edges (applied after the safe area), with 0 as the new default where the badge used to float 10 to 14 points off the corner depending on its size preset. The `attributionSettings(size:position:margin:textColor:)` builders on the view and on the settings gained the parameter, so the old look is one `margin: 12` away. Corners of the plate that sit on a view edge render square, and only the corners facing the map interior keep the radius, so the pinned badge reads as a corner strip instead of a pill notched into the corner; a badge that floats (a margin, or a safe-area inset) keeps all four rounded corners.

- The tile source is one URL template: `ImmersiveMapView().tileURLTemplate("https://tiles.com/{x}/{y}/{z}?apiKey=xxx")` points the map at any MVT endpoint. The `{x}`/`{y}`/`{z}` placeholders may appear in any order and the query string is preserved as written, so a key can live in the template; credentials that travel as HTTP headers go in the modifier's `headers` parameter, which adds them to every tile request (offline region downloads included), `Authorization` included. The source is only where bytes come from: how they are parsed and drawn is configured separately through the style, where `VectorTileMapStyle` now carries the `ImmersiveMapVectorTileLabelProfile` (`VectorTileMapStyle(style:labelProfile:)`) so a non-OpenMapTiles schema pairs the template with its own style and label keys. A set template wins over both the base-URL path (no `/{z}/{x}/{y}.mvt` is appended) and TileJSON discovery; a template malformed enough to not form an absolute URL falls back to the base path. The template and the header names are part of the disk-cache identity; header values are deliberately not, so rotating a key keeps the caches warm. The new `ImmersiveMapTilesService` enum holds the hosted-service constants (base URL, TileJSON URL, maximum zoom, attribution) that a bare `ImmersiveMapView()` renders with. `ImmersiveMapCustomTilesMac` now wires a template, headers, a custom style with a label profile and an attribution end to end.

- Camera constraints are fully configurable, where two of them used to be hardcoded. `CameraSettings.minimumPitch` sets a tilt floor, so a map can be kept perpetually tilted instead of always allowing the straight-down view, and `CameraSettings.maximumAbsoluteBearing` caps how far the camera may rotate away from north, where rotation on the flat map was unbounded. Both compose with the globe's zoom-eased limits rather than fighting them: the bearing cap is the widest the globe's bearing window opens instead of the full half turn, and a tilt floor above the globe's zoomed-out ceiling yields to it, so a whole-globe view still levels off. New `ImmersiveMapView` modifiers `.pitchRange(minimum:maximum:)` and `.bearingLimit(_:)` sit next to `.zoomRange(minimum:maximum:)`. The read side follows: `ImmersiveMapCameraSnapshot.pitchLimits.minimum` reports the floor in force instead of a constant zero, the built-in control panel sizes its tilt slider from it, and the one-thumb tilt zone maps its drag to the reachable span rather than leaving a dead stretch. `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac` gains a Camera sidebar section with every limit on a live slider; the zoom range moved there from the Presentation section.

- `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` collects the third-party license notices in one place: the ISC notice for the Mapbox earcut code the internal triangulator is ported from (previously stated only in the source file header), ready to copy into an app's acknowledgements screen. The README's license section links it, and its app-size table no longer names SwiftEarcut, a dependency the internal port replaced.

- New example `Examples/macOS/ImmersiveMapMac`: the plain map on macOS, with the built-in tile provider, `.enableCameraUIControls()` and `.debugPanel()` and nothing else on screen. Every other Mac example documents one API and puts a panel of switches over the map; this one is the map itself, which is what to open to fly around, to read the HUD while something in the engine is being changed, or to see what the defaults look like before any setting is touched.

- Marked pedestrian crossings draw as zebra stripes. The tiles ship a crossing as the footway line that runs across the carriageway, which states where it is, which way it faces and how long it is, and from z15 they state whether it is painted (`crossing`, `markings`); a crossing the ground actually carries (`marked`, `traffic_signals`, or any stated marking pattern) now draws as a band of white stripes on the asphalt, sized in metres and laid along the crossing so the stripes run with the traffic. An `unmarked` or undescribed crossing draws nothing, which is the honest reading of a tag that says a crossing exists but not that it is painted. The stripes draw in the automobile tier above every carriageway and fade in on the markings' camera-zoom band with the rest of the paint.

- Road markings fade in with the camera rather than appearing with a tile level. `LowZoomOverviewFade.roadMarkingAlpha` brings every marking in continuously between camera zoom 14 and 15, so nothing pops when the engine swaps the tile level serving a street. The band overlaps the one the carriageway widths morph over (14 to 16), which is visible on the widest carriageways only: a lane line's offset is measured against the true carriageway, so while a road is still drawn at its symbol width the outermost line sits outside the drawn edge, by up to two and a half points on an eight-lane one-way around zoom 14.5. Roads of four lanes and fewer are unaffected, and in central Moscow one-way carriageways of six lanes or more are 1.4% of the streets that carry paint at all.

- A one-way carriageway carries a line on every boundary between its lanes, which the lane count alone fixes: every lane on it runs the same way, so no knowledge of where each lane leads is needed. A two-way street is painted down the middle and nowhere else, and only where its lanes divide evenly: which of them run each way is `lanes:forward`/`lanes:backward`, mapped on a few per cent of streets, so an odd total leaves the centre inside a driving lane rather than between two, and the street stays bare (about seven per cent of the painted streets in a city centre).

- The gap a marking leaves at a junction is sized by the road it meets. It used to be half of the marking's own carriageway, so a lane line running into a six-lane avenue stopped half a lane short of it and painted over the avenue; the inset is now half of the widest carriageway that meets the point.

- Markings follow what the tiles say about the road, not only its lane count. A source that states where the count came from (`lanes_src`: `tagged` from the data, `assumed` from the class) is painted only where it was mapped, so a road drawn at a default width no longer implies markings nobody surveyed; a source that ships no such field is read as before. A road whose `surface` the tiles call `unpaved` draws no paint at all, since there is none on gravel, and a surface value the tiles do not classify says nothing either way and stays painted.

- Markings read which street a piece of road belongs to from the source where it says so. A tiler that assembles streets from the whole network before cutting tiles can state it (`street`, an id that holds across a tile boundary and tells two same-named streets in different towns apart), and the engine then knows a junction from a seam exactly instead of comparing names and drawing attributes: a street that continues carries its paint through, and two streets that meet break it. The same id is what stitches a street's pieces into one ribbon, which now works even where the tiler wrote their lane counts differently; what still has to agree is everything that changes how a piece is drawn, so a street running into a tunnel and out again stays two ribbons. A source without the field behaves as before.

- A dash pattern broken at a junction resumes in step on the far side. Every tessellated piece used to start counting arc length at zero, so each block of a street began with a fresh full stroke wherever the line had been cut, and one street came out as strokes of assorted lengths. Pieces now carry how far along the line they begin, the inset included.

- A junction reconstructed from the road graph (`origin=graph` on the tile feature) wears exactly the class colour, one asphalt with the ribbons and the hand-mapped areas. It briefly drew half a tone lighter within this unreleased line, and the tone turned out to draw every overlap between reconstructed polygons, hand-mapped areas and ribbons as a diagonal seam; what sets a crossing apart is not a colour but that no lane paint runs inside it.

### Changed

- The built-in style draws the overview road network as a veil in one asphalt grey, the same grey the street era draws the class in at a little over half opacity, so the network sits in the land rather than on it and the ground colours stay the picture. Through tile z11 every drive tier that draws (motorways and trunks from z5, primaries from z7, secondaries from z9, tertiaries from z10) is a point-locked stroke with round joins and round caps and without a kerb: rank reads as width alone, stepping up over three bands (country, region, city view), and the symbol era now runs through tile z11 instead of z9, so the city view no longer shows every class as the same tile-scaled hairline. The tiles ship a corridor in many short features, and the butt ends and plain joins of the previous stroke tore it open at every bend and feature boundary; round geometry closes every seam. The street era from z12 draws opaque as before. Each class fades in with the camera over the zoom level after the tile zoom that first ships it (motorways from 5, trunks from 6, primaries from 7, secondaries from 9, tertiaries from 10) instead of popping with the tile: the stroke bakes a per-class fade mask that the flat and the globe atlas path both evaluate against the live camera zoom. The dark slate motorway accent of the country view is gone with it.

- A vehicular tunnel is a bare translucent fill. The built-in style used to dash a `brunnel=tunnel` ribbon, and at street zoom, where the tiles ship the tunnel's road surface as a polygon, the tunnel vanished altogether: the polygon carries the tunnel's `layer` but not its `brunnel`, so it drew as open asphalt with a kerb, the shipped lane paint drew across it, and the only feature that said tunnel, the centreline, was clipped away inside that very surface. Now the parser stamps a negative-layer road surface as a tunnel when a `brunnel=tunnel` centreline of the same street (or one running inside the polygon) is in the tile, which keeps a street passing under a bridge, `layer=-1` and in full view, out of it; the style draws every tunnel, ribbon or surface, as the class colour at twenty percent opacity (`ImmersiveMapTilesDefaultMapStyle.tunnelFillOpacity`) with no dash, no kerb and no paint, and the parser clips the shipped marking lines out of tunnel surfaces. Tunnel geometry draws under style keys of its own (a style key is one baked colour per tile, and a translucent tunnel sharing its class key made the first tunnel in a tile the colour of every road of that class), and `layer` and `street` are read whatever integer field the tile writer chose (`sint` in the ImmersiveMap pipeline). A tunnel ribbon ends flat rather than in a round cap: the centreline overshoots its surface into the portal by a few units, and the cap on the stub the surface leaves showed as a darker semicircle over the tunnel. The road the tunnel passes under is untouched: it is drawn over the tunnel as before. The prepared tile cache format moved to 72 so tiles baked with the old look rebuild. New visual review scenario `roads.tunnel.arbat` (the Boulevard Ring under Novy Arbat).

- The text shader skips the halo outright when a style asks for none, instead of differencing two distance fields. The fill is read from the MSDF and the outer edge from the plain SDF, and at zero width their difference is not reliably zero, so a hairline of stroke colour could survive around a corner. No built-in label class asks for a zero halo; a custom style that does now gets a clean glyph.

- A POI label is three quarters of the size it was, and a POI with no icon is not labelled at all. The icon disc, the name and the gap between them are laid out as one block scaled by a single factor, which was 1.4: the disc came out forty points across and the name as large as a city's, so the POIs read as buttons dropped onto the map and a long museum name became a paragraph over the buildings. The factor is now 1.05, so the disc is thirty points and the name a shade under twelve, a step below a city's. Separately, a POI whose category the icon set does not recognize (an office, a company, a monument, a named building, and every other class outside the fourteen the sprite sheet covers) used to draw as bare text from `poiIconlessMinimumZoom`; those names are the bulk of a city centre's POIs and say nothing about what the place is, so they are now left out. The new `LabelVisibility.poiRequiresIcon` carries that decision (`true` by default) and `poiIconlessMinimumZoom` is read only when it is `false`, which restores the old behaviour exactly. Prepared-tile format version 69, since the label geometry is baked.

- The built-in attribution badge reads "© OpenStreetMap". It read "© OpenStreetMap © OpenMapTiles", which named the data and the schema, from when the hosted planet was an OpenFreeMap build in the OpenMapTiles schema. The tiles are now built by the ImmersiveMap tile pipeline in a schema of its own, so OpenStreetMap is the only party the licence asks to be named, and the badge says exactly that. The link still leads to openstreetmap.org/copyright, `ImmersiveMapTilesService.attribution` keeps its shape and only its `title` changes, and a source you point the map at yourself still owns its own credit. `ATTRIBUTION.md`, the map data and custom tile source pages, the README and the `ImmersiveMapCustomTilesMac` example carry the same text.

- A street draws the same markings at tile zoom 15 as at 16. The measured streetscape ships from z15 (junction and carriageway surfaces, parking lots, the centre and edge lines), but four figures painted on those surfaces were held back to z16: the lane separators, the zebra crossings, the letter A of a bus lane and the sawtooth of its stop, and the parking bay comb. A camera between zoom 15 and 16 is served z15 tiles, so a street sat there as bare asphalt with a centre line and then gained its whole paint job in one step when the camera crossed 16 and the tile level swapped under it. All four now draw from the level the surface they are painted on ships at, which is the level the road graph itself ships at, and one constant (`streetDetailMinimumTileZoom`) states it instead of three. How small a figure is allowed to get on screen is decided where it always was, once, by the camera-zoom band the paint fades in on (`LowZoomOverviewFade.roadMarkingStartZoom`, camera zoom 15 to 15.4): that band is continuous in the camera, so the paint arrives by fading rather than by a tile-level step. Nothing changes for a source that ships the fine markings only at z16, and the tiles have to ship them at z15 for the change to be visible; the ImmersiveMap tile pipeline does so from the same build. New visual review scenario `roads.osm2streets.tverskaya.z15` holds the junction one level out against the z16 shot. Prepared-tile format version 67.

- A road whose tiles state its width is drawn at that width. The geometry layer may now carry `width`, the measured breadth of the whole carriageway in decimetres, and the built-in style prefers it to the lane-count estimate (implausible values outside 2 to 60 metres are ignored). The point of the field is agreement: when the width is produced by the same cross-section model that reconstructs junction polygons, the ribbons entering a junction and the junction surface itself share one width model, so the surface meets the ribbon mouths flush instead of bulging past them or falling short. A stated width that changes along a street is a real fact about the ground (a turn pocket opening before a junction), so `width` joins the attributes the street stitcher refuses to weld across, where a differing lane count is treated as tiler noise and bridged. Prepared-tile format version 55.

- Roads morph from symbol to surface with the camera instead of doubling at every tile level. A road drawn at its true carriageway width doubles on screen with every zoom, because the ground does; that is honest and reads badly between z13 and z15, where a map wants a street to be a readable symbol of steady weight. Each drive-tier class now carries a symbol ceiling in points (motorway 7, trunk 6.5, primary 6, secondary 5, tertiary 4.5, minor 4, service 2.5; the kerb one point wider), and the ground shader morphs the visible edge from the symbol to the true width over camera zoom 14 to 16 (`LowZoomOverviewFade.roadSurfaceBlend`, continuous, so a tile-level swap changes nothing about what is drawn). `TileLineStyle` gained `maximumWidthPoints` in its second reserved slot.

- One-way carriageways carry lane lines. A one-way road of several lanes (most of central Moscow's avenues, now that the tiles carry `oneway`) gets a dashed line on each boundary between lanes, the centreline offset sideways to each boundary with mitred corners, and no centre divider; a single-lane one-way is bare. Two-way streets keep the centre divider.

- Ribbons inside a carriageway surface are clipped away. Where the tiles ship a junction area, the ribbon of the street it covers runs inside it (Mokhovaya 12 of 17 points, Tverskaya 13 of 17, Okhotny Ryad entirely, in the tile that shows it); drawn there, the ribbon's fill painted over the surface's kerb along one side, leaving a street with a kerb on one side and none on the other. A ribbon is now cut at the outline of any surface of its own or a higher class and draws only outside it, ending flush at the edge; the surface owns that ground. Prepared-tile format version 46.

- Junction areas draw as carriageway surfaces. A `transportation` polygon the tiles ship with `subclass=junction_area` (the junction as OSM maps it, `area:highway`) now draws in the automobile road tier as the surface of its road class with a kerb on its outline, sorted among the roads of that class, so the ribbons entering the junction merge into one surface and their kerbs stop at its edge instead of crossing it; before, such a polygon fell to the ground layer under every road. Prepared-tile format version 45.

- The engine's side of the road-network contract is in place ahead of the tiles. When the geometry layer carries a street `name`, pieces of one street (same name, class, subclass, lanes, oneway, brunnel and layer) that meet at an endpoint exactly two drive-tier features share are stitched into one polyline before tessellation, so the street is one ribbon with no seam where the pieces met; an endpoint a third road touches is a junction and is never stitched across, and pieces without a name draw exactly as before. When it carries `oneway`, a one-way carriageway gets no centre divider. Both wait on the tile pipeline shipping those fields and change nothing on today's tiles.

- Round line joins follow the rim arc. A round join was one triangle between the two outer corners of the bend, which leaves a circular segment of sagitta R(1 - cos(turn/2)) uncovered: invisible on the 20-unit ribbons roads used to be, and a bite of several metres out of the outside corner of a 48 m carriageway at a right angle now that roads are drawn at their real width. The join is now a fan that follows the arc in steps of at most 20 degrees (a gentle bend stays one triangle), with every rim vertex on the arc; the cap tessellation already did this. Prepared-tile format version 44. The road-network data contract the tiles need for clean junctions (one feature per street, no lane-count steps at turn pockets, `oneway` and `name` in the geometry layer, junction areas, snapped shared endpoints) is written up as a contract for the tile pipeline, against the live tile that shows each problem.

- Long labels wrap to six lines in a wider box instead of trailing off the third. A point label wraps at a box ten ems wide to at most three lines; one whose name did not fit had its remainder run unwrapped along the third line, so a long institution name read as a two-word stack with a sentence hanging off it. A name that overflows the base lines is now wrapped again at a box one and a half times as wide and up to six lines, reading as a compact paragraph; short names keep the base box exactly as before. The collision box follows the laid-out size, so a taller label claims its room. Prepared-tile format version 43, since the label geometry is baked into prepared tiles.

- Roads are drawn at their real carriageway width, and automobile roads carry lane markings. The built-in style stated road widths directly in tile units, which meant the same street covered a different amount of ground at every tile zoom: with tiles now shipping to z16, one street drawn from a coarse tile in the distance came out twice as wide as itself from the next zoom's tile and four times as wide as from the one after, and none of those widths was the road's own. A width is now a fact about the ground, stated in metres and converted per tile from its zoom and latitude, so a street holds one width wherever it is drawn. The metres come from the `lanes` count the tiles carry (a six-lane avenue is six lanes wide, roughly 21 m), falling back to a typical lane count per class where the tag is absent, and the point floors still carry each class through the zooms where its true width is sub-pixel. The casing became a kerb while it was there: a fixed margin of ground on each side instead of a fraction of the road, which as a fraction was invisible on a symbolic width and metres wide on a true one. From tile zoom 15 every drive-tier road except service alleys carries a dashed lane divider, painted in the `detail` road pass above every carriageway, point-locked so it stays a marking rather than growing into a second road. The divider's ribbon is tessellated tight (8 tile units per point, against the 32 of an overview line: on a z15+ tile a unit is centimetres, so the stroke is still hosted with margin) with round joins, and the tessellator's round-join fan now carries the join's own arc length instead of a constant, so a dash that spans a bend paints the corner wedge; before, every bend in a dashed marking showed a notch the exact shape of the gap between the two segment rectangles, and a wide ribbon made the notch long. The divider is paint on the ground, not a screen pattern: its dash period is a length in metres (three of paint, six of gap) converted to the tile's units and flagged world-locked, and the ground shader cuts a world-locked pattern from arc length without the point-to-unit conversion a border's dashes get, so the dashes sit still on the asphalt and keep their count while the camera zooms or the engine swaps the tile level serving the road; only the stroke width stays point-locked, a hairline of paint at every zoom. Internally `TileLineStyle` gained `dashInTileUnits` in its first reserved slot, which is a prepared-tile format change (version 41). Markings draw from tile zoom 13, and stop half a carriageway short of a road's ends and of every junction (a tile-seam cut is not an end and keeps running flush into the neighbour), so the last dash never pokes past the fill at a dead end and the divider never runs across the street it meets. The automobile network draws as its own tier above the pedestrian one: a fourth road structure, `automobileGround`, sits between the ground and the bridges in the draw and arena order, and the parser routes every road whose class priority is at or above the service tier into it, so a footway ending against an avenue no longer lies over its kerb and a path crossing a street no longer cuts the street's casing (prepared-tile format version 42). Plain pedestrian paths wear the ground color with no kerb and no dashes: over land they are the ground itself, over a park or a square a pale route across it, rather than a second beige road system laid over the map. Lane widths count the whole street, not only the painted lane: a counted lane carries its share of the parking strip and gutter (4 m on the drive tiers, 5 m on minor streets), since OSM's `lanes` is the marked through lanes and a two-lane city street with parked cars is around 12 m, not 6.5. New visual review scenario `roads.carriageways.street`; the style implementation revision is bumped, so cached prepared tiles re-prepare on first launch.

- Country-view landcover no longer reads as camouflage. Over a farmed plain (Central Russia at z5: cropland 65% of the tile, forest 29%) everything rendered as four near-identical greens: the low-zoom vegetation blend, built to merge raster blobs into one mass on the globe, was still half strength at z5, tinting even the neutral ground green while leaving the blob edges visible as blotches. Three coordinated changes: farmland (and its overview `crop` biome, one color by the palette invariant) is now a pale wheat instead of a green, so the dominant land class recedes into the ground family and forests become the one green drawn on it; the vegetation blend releases right after the globe (full merge through z2, 0.5 at z3, 0.15 at z5, gone by z8) instead of lingering at half strength through the country zooms; and regional (admin 3-4) borders at overview zooms (z <= 6) lighten and drop to half opacity, so oblast outlines stop scribbling the one cold saturated hue across the frame, while national borders keep their full weight. New visual review scenario `landcover.overview.plain` holds the farmed-plain case; the style implementation revision is bumped, so cached prepared tiles re-prepare on first launch. Overview line weights follow the lightened ground, and the overview roads draw on the country-border principle end to end: one point-locked line pass in the generic ground path (motorway 1.5 points, trunk 1.4, primary 1.1, secondary 0.9), opaque from the first frame they are visible, with butt ends and plain joins, and a ribbon tessellated wide enough to actually host the stated points (the shader clamps a point width to the tessellated ribbon, and overview ribbons used to be the sub-pixel world width, which silently collapsed every requested width to a hairline; border ribbons are re-provisioned the same way so their 1.6/1.1 points materialize too). Two road-specific behaviors are gone from the overview era because they composited unevenly: the z5-6 road fade band, which held the skeleton translucent exactly at the zooms it is the star, and the wide round caps, whose translucent overlaps darkened every joint; tunnels and construction segments read as point-dashed corridors instead. From z10 the world width takes over with the point floors as a safety net, so street zooms are untouched.

- Label priority over the built-in tile schema follows the one-number `rank` contract the tiles now bake: lower is more important, and a feature without a `rank` is the least important thing in its layer. The tiles now bake population and capital status into the rank at build time, so the profile's old fallbacks are gone: `capital` no longer floats a rankless place to the top and `population` no longer synthesizes an importance (the two mechanisms used to disagree, putting every region name above every city and tying Moscow with Kaluga). The capital bold-and-larger treatment in the built-in style is now `class == "city" && rank == 1`, which is what the contract says a national capital looks like, and a POI without a rank reveals at the tail of the overzoom schedule instead of mid-tail. Priorities are baked into prepared label runs, so the prepared-tile format version is bumped and cached tiles re-prepare.

- Automobile roads are asphalt grey, and importance reads as width. The drive tiers of the built-in style's road palette (`motorway` through `service` on `RoadLayerStyles.immersiveMapTilesDefault`) trade the gold-to-white ramp for a single cool neutral asphalt grey in the manner of a driving map, one road surface across the whole network, so the rest of the palette keeps its light, warm, system-map character. With color out of the hierarchy, the width ramp is spread wider: motorways grow from 16 to 20 tile units and trunks to 17.5, primaries and secondaries gain a step, minor and service roads give a little back, so a major reads as a major by being wider, not darker. The motorway and trunk overview accents baked for country views become a deeper asphalt instead of a deep orange, released to the street palette by the same camera-zoom blend, and the casing the style derives from a fill is now a uniform step down instead of a warm-biased one, so the neutral fills keep their hue in the edge. Footpaths, tracks, rail and ferries are untouched; a changed default configuration changes the style fingerprint, and the implementation revision is bumped for the width, accent and casing changes, so cached prepared tiles re-prepare on the first launch.

- The default palette is lighter, warmer and lower in contrast, in the manner of the system maps people already know. The built-in style's ground is a warm off-white instead of a cool near-white grey, the greens are soft pastels, water is a clearer light blue, minor streets are white with a whisper of a warm-grey casing instead of grey lines with a dark border, and the road hierarchy reads as warmth (a warm gold on motorways, pale yellow primaries, white side streets) rather than as darkness; buildings are a warm light grey a step under the ground, so a roof separates from the street around it. The overview biomes of the globe are the same colors class by class, so nothing shifts hue while zooming, and the residential and industrial land uses sit a hair under the ground instead of greying whole districts. The engine defaults that stand in for the ground before tiles arrive follow the palette: `SceneSettings.mapClearColor` and `StyleSettings.baseColors.tileBackground` are now the style's land and `baseColors.water` its water, so a still-loading tile, the horizon haze, the placeholder globe and the polar cap wear the ground the tiles paint over them (all three used to be a different white and blue). The road casing the style derives from a fill is a smaller, warmer step down. The style's implementation revision is bumped, so cached prepared tiles re-prepare on the first launch.

- Buildings are solid by default. `StyleSettings.buildingExtrusionMode` defaults to `.solid` (both on `ImmersiveMapSettings.default` and in the memberwise initializer), where it used to be `.translucent`: a translucent roof composites over the ground under it, which its own building shadows, so with shadows on every roof came out a muddy grey darker than its own color, and the whole city read as a field of grey slabs. Opaque massing keeps roofs light and walls shaded, the way a lit city reads, and is depth-correct against scene models. `.translucent` and `.solidAtHighZoom` are unchanged and one modifier away; a new visual review scenario, `buildings.translucent`, keeps the composited path looked at.

- Shadows are softer, and the walls carry a wider tonal range. `ShadowSettings.strength` defaults to 0.22 instead of 0.5, which with the new cool tint puts a fully shadowed white surface at a light blue-grey rather than a mid grey, so a shadowed street still reads as daylight; the earlier look is `strength: 0.5, tint: SIMD3<Float>(repeating: 1)`. On the extruded buildings, the tonal cue that separates faces without an analytic light widened: even a wall square to the sun now stays a step under the roof (so the roof edge never dissolves into a lit facade), the swing between the sun-facing and the far side of a building grew from three to seven percent, and the ground gradient deepened a touch, so roof, lit wall, side wall and shaded wall are four distinct tones of one color.

- The hosted tile service moved: tiles are served from `https://immersivemap.dev/tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}.mvt`, and the old `tiles.immersivemap.dev` host is gone. `ImmersiveMapTilesService.tileBaseURL`, every demo app's template, and the docs all point at the new path, and the changed base URL re-keys the disk caches on its own, so nothing stale survives the move. The new endpoint serves no TileJSON document, so the shipped defaults no longer configure a TileJSON URL: the loader goes straight to the base path instead of firing a discovery request that can only 404 (custom sources can still set `NetworkSettings.tileJSONURL`).

- The demo apps read a developer's tile API key from two local-only places, so a real key never has to touch a committed file: the `IMMERSIVEMAP_API_KEY` environment variable (every example, post and visual review scheme carries it with an empty value as a placeholder), and, when that is empty, a gitignored `LocalSecrets.plist` at the repository root with the same string key, created once with `/usr/libexec/PlistBuddy -c "Add :IMMERSIVEMAP_API_KEY string im_YOUR_KEY" LocalSecrets.plist`. The plist is found by walking up from the source file's path, which covers the Mac and the simulator; a physical device cannot read files off the Mac, so the two device-capable projects (`ImmersiveMapIOS`, `ImmersiveMapVisualReviewIOS`) copy the plist into the app bundle in a "Bundle LocalSecrets" build phase and the key lookup falls back to the bundled copy there. Builds made with the key baked in are for the developer's own device, not for distribution.

- Admin boundaries hold a designed width in layout points instead of pumping with the zoom. Line widths are baked into tile geometry in tile units, and the engine covers the fraction between integer zooms by moving the camera, so every baked width grew up to twice its size within a zoom level and snapped back when the next level's tiles arrived; borders also stepped through a hand-tuned per-tile-zoom width table. A style can now declare `lineWidthPoints`: the tile shader already reconstructs the line edge from the vertices' distance field, so it resolves the visible edge in screen space each frame from the field's per-pixel gradient, continuously in camera zoom, with the tessellated width acting only as the ceiling the edge can move inside (requests past it clamp half a pixel inside the rim and stay feathered). Both built-in styles opt their admin boundaries in at 1.4 points for country borders and 0.8 for regional ones; roads keep the world-locked behavior, which is the designed look at street level. The first-party style's boundary dashes also switch from round caps to feathered butt cuts, since caps ate into the dash gaps by their radius, and its per-zoom boundary width table is gone. On the globe, where tiles bake into cached atlas pages that the fractional-zoom dolly then magnifies on screen, the bake converts points to texels through each slot's own texel-per-pixel ratio, log-quantized into the atlas redraw hash: the page re-bakes in roughly nine-percent steps as the dolly moves, so the on-screen width holds there too, and stays continuous across an integer zoom because the next level's halved demand lands on the same quantization grid. Point-locked widths also taper by camera zoom, half size over a planet view easing to full size by regional zoom, because a width designed for a region view is far too heavy on a z1 globe; the taper is continuous in camera zoom (folded into the atlas's quantized re-bake scale on the globe), so it cannot reintroduce the jumps the point lock removed. The first-party style additionally calms its boundaries at planet zooms: regional (admin 3-4) borders, which the tile source ships from z1 and which covered the globe in purple dots, are hidden below tile zoom 4; country borders stay dashed at every zoom, since the point-locked pattern keeps its designed dash size even over a planet view. Tiles at z0-z2 wrap so much of the sphere that any tile-averaged scale magnifies lines at the center of the globe face, about twice at z0, fading by z3, which made borders visibly change size across those levels; a coarse-tile compensation on the globe path, a function of the source tile zoom only, evens them out.

- The dash pattern is stated in points and cut in the shader. Dashes used to be cut into the geometry at parse time, in tile units, so their length was an accident of the tile grid, and at the widths borders now hold they smeared into a wobbly quasi-solid line. A line style can now state its dash pattern in layout points (`dashLengthPoints`/`dashGapPoints`): such a line tessellates as one continuous ribbon whose vertices carry arc length, and the fragment shader cuts the pattern with the same one-pixel antialiasing ramp on the cuts as on the sides. The period is anchored to the geometry, not the screen: it converts the points into tile units once per draw at the tile's nominal display scale (a constant of the tile and the viewport), because a period that follows the live camera makes every dash boundary sit at a multiple of a moving number and the whole pattern crawls along the line during panning, rotation and zoom. Anchored, the dashes stay glued to the map, sized from the styled points at each level's native scale, and breathe with the fractional zoom like every other map feature. The first-party boundary dashes moved to this (7 points of dash, 3.5 of gap), which also shrinks their geometry to a fraction of the pre-cut version. Per-style line parameters (width, dash pattern, edge threshold) now travel as one `TileLineStyle` span per geometry layer, the edge threshold left the vertex for it, and the freed byte rejoined the longitudinal vertex parameter as a 16-bit value; the prepared-tile disk format is version 36.

- Region views show cities and calmer terrain. The WorldCover `urban` class, which the style used to hide, renders as a soft warm gray from its first tile, handing over to the OSM residential beige from z10, so a metropolis reads as a distinct patch instead of a pale hole in the green. The vegetation classes also blend further toward the shared tone through the mid zooms (70 percent at z3 easing to 20 at z8), calming the raster-derived crop-and-grass blobs that dominated the region view. Road accents and width floors are unchanged.

- Roads start at tile z5 and rivers are visible from their first tile. The motorway skeleton (with its construction corridors) appears from tile z5, fading in over camera z5-z6; the source adds trunk-class geometry at z6 and primary at z7, so a corridor whose OSM tagging changes class shows cut until its class arrives, a property of the data. Waterways, which the source ships from z3, carry a width floor (0.7 points for rivers and canals, 0.5 for streams) so the major rivers read as thin threads over a country view instead of dimming into invisibility; their world growth takes over toward street zoom exactly like the road floors.

- Construction road segments draw again, as dashed corridors. The OpenMapTiles source ships `motorway_construction` (and the other `_construction` variants) from the same zoom as the base class, and the road policy's unknown-class default hid them until street zoom, cutting corridors mid-line over a country view. A construction class now gates and colors like its base class and draws point-dashed, without casing or the motorway accent: a corridor that exists but is not finished. (The other cuts visible at z4-z5 are the data itself: the source carries only motorway-class geometry there, and a corridor tagged trunk joins at z6, primary at z7.)

- Roads read as roads at region zooms. Three changes in the first-party style, on top of the class-by-zoom policy: major road widths carry a point floor (motorway 1.4, trunk 1.3, primary 1.1, secondary 0.9, tertiary 0.8, rail 0.7), applied only while the world-locked width is below it, so the majors hold readable strokes over a country view instead of dimming into sub-pixel hairlines, and still grow with the world at street level; motorways and trunks wear a deeper orange accent over the overview, baked as their color with the light street palette as its street counterpart, so the accent releases through the same continuous camera-zoom blend as the ground palette; and casing joins a class only from tile z10, where the fill is about two points wide and an edge can actually render, so below that the majors draw as clean single strokes instead of muddying their own antialiasing with a sub-pixel casing. The prepared-tile format is version 38: the line-style span widened for the width floor, with reserved space.

- Border width no longer depends on where the camera looks. On the globe, point-locked widths converted points to texels through each atlas slot's projected screen footprint, which is a camera quantity: panning or tilting compressed a tile's footprint and its borders re-baked visibly thicker, one tile at a time. The conversion now uses only constants of the slot and the viewport plus one global, quantized fractional-zoom dolly factor, identical for every tile: pan, tilt, rotation, limb position and even atlas budget downgrades cannot change a line's width anymore, and the dolly factor lands exactly on one at integer zooms, so widths stay continuous across level crossings too.

- One palette at every zoom. The default style used two ground palettes, the saturated overview set on the globe and the pale street set near the ground, and even a perfectly smooth handover between them meant the map changed its colors while zooming. The default overview palette now IS the street palette, class by class: the same pale water blue, the same near-white land base and the same greens from the whole planet down to the street, so nothing shifts hue on the way. The polar cap's water default follows, keeping the ocean seamless at the cap. The two-palette machinery stays: a custom `ImmersiveMapTilesDefaultMapStyleConfiguration` may still differentiate the overview and street sets and gets the smooth camera-zoom handover between them.

- The ground palette hands over continuously with the camera instead of flipping at a zoom boundary. Crossing tile z9 to z10 used to switch the background from the overview land tone to the near-white street land, the water from the saturated globe blue to the pale street blue, and the WorldCover biome greens ceased to exist at the same boundary (the tile service merges them only through z9): the whole map snapped from a green world to a white one in one step, and any per-tile-zoom bridge just trades the cliff for smaller steps. Ground styles now bake both colors, the overview one and its street counterpart, in every tile, and the shader lerps between them with a per-frame blend driven by camera zoom (smooth over z8 to z11.5), so the rendered color is identical on both sides of every tile swap. Each WorldCover biome's street color is the OSM landcover class that replaces it, and the replacing class enters wearing the biome's overview color, so at the z9/z10 data handover only the polygon shapes change, never the color language. The prepared-tile format is version 37: the style span carries the color pair.

- Roads follow a low-zoom policy and their casing sits under the fill at every zoom. Below the separate-road zoom (tile z8) roads render through the generic ground path, which draws by ascending style key, and the casing keys sorted above the fills: every road showed as its darker casing at 1.5x width with the colored fill hidden underneath. The first-party casing keys moved below the fills, restoring the intended layering (casing first, fill overdraws) on both paths. The same style also stops drawing every road class the tile ships at overview zooms, where they are all sub-pixel hairlines that grey the map: motorways and trunks carry the country view (easing in through the engine's z3 to z4 road fade band, which the style now opts into), primary joins at tile z7, secondary at z9, tertiary and rail at z10, minor at z12, service at z13.

- Low-zoom landcover is calmer. The WorldCover-derived vegetation classes rendered at full contrast from z3, where the raster-derived polygons read as blotches; the massive-overview merge (fully shared tone through z2) now releases gradually, 65 percent at z3 easing to the unmixed palette by z9, with forests keeping a quarter of their distance throughout as before. Deserts and snow stay unblended: those are real geographic edges. Also fixed: the synthetic per-tile background quad was always styled as a zoom-0 tile, so the overview grass tone painted under the whole map at every zoom and the land base colors were dead code; it now carries the real tile and the zoom-banded background works as written.

- Vector lines (admin boundaries, roads, rivers, rails) are antialiased analytically. The tessellator extrudes every line two tile units past its styled width and writes a signed distance-from-centerline field into the vertices, and the tile fragment shader resolves the styled edge from that field with a one-pixel smoothstep ramp, the same construction the route ribbon already used. This is the first edge antialiasing lines get on the globe at all: in spherical presentation tiles are rasterized into non-MSAA atlas pages, so until now every border and road on the globe had hard, stair-stepped edges, and the whole-unit vertex quantization made thin borders visibly ripple in width. The coverage now follows the interpolated field rather than the snapped triangle edges, which smooths both. Lines thinner than a pixel fade instead of breaking into dots, and the flat map's MSAA gets the same ramp on top. The field is two-dimensional: a second, longitudinal distance feathers free butt ends, so dash cuts and line terminations get the same one-pixel ramp as the sides, while cuts that must stay hard to meet adjacent geometry flush (tile seams, road junctions, capped ends) keep it saturated and render unchanged. Plain polygon fills are untouched, marked by a zero edge threshold in the vertex. The prepared-tile disk format is version 34: the vertex padding bytes now carry the distance fields, so cached tiles from earlier versions re-prepare on first launch. A new visual review scenario, `lines.globe.borders`, holds the case that motivated this (country borders and hairline roads on the sphere at a continental zoom).

- Labels are sized in layout points instead of drawable pixels, so they read at the same physical size on every display. Label text, halos, POI icons and the collision grids were constants in device pixels, converted nowhere: a 3x iPhone rendered a label pixel-identically to a 2x Mac, which made it two thirds the point size and about half the physical size, and packed 2.25 times as many labels into each unit of perceived screen area. Routes had already made this decision (`ImmersiveMapRoute.widthPoints`, converted with `pixelsPerPoint` at the render boundary); labels now follow it. A style value is a point value, the glyph and icon geometry a prepared tile carries is baked in points and therefore stays independent of the display it was built on, and the multiply happens once per frame, in the label vertex and placement shaders, from `FrameContext.screenScale`. Halo widths became em ratios (`haloEm`) rather than absolute widths, which is what stops the smallest label class carrying the widest halo relative to its strokes. Halos therefore track the text now, so a class whose size differs from the one its ratio was derived from draws a different halo than before even at the reference scale: an ocean label, three points larger than the water class its ratio comes from, gets a proportionally wider one. The public style types changed with the unit: `ImmersiveMapLabelTextStyle` and both provider configurations now take `haloEm` and `sizePoints` in place of `strokeWidthPx` and `sizePx`, and `LabelSettings.base/road.gridCellSizePx` is now `gridCellSizePoints` (default 16, the point equivalent of the 32 pixels it used to be at the 2x reference), exposed as a slider in `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac`.

- Label sizes that fell under the platform floor for readable type are raised to it. Converting the built-in palettes at the 2x scale they were authored against put POI labels at 8 points, water at 9.5 and house numbers at 6, well below what a phone can present legibly, so `LabelTypeScale.minimumSizePoints` (11) now clamps every style the providers produce. Only the classes that were under the floor moved; the rest keep the sizes they had. House numbers are the visible one: at 11 points they are readable and the collision grid thins them out, where before there were more of them and none could be read. The POI sprite atlas cell went from 64 to 128 pixels so an icon on a 3x phone is not magnified further than it was before.

- Video export renders at a pixels-per-point derived from the output size instead of a fixed 1, and SwiftUI markers rasterize at that same scale. A marker view is laid out in points exactly like a label, so a fixed rasterization scale would have shrunk it against the map: at 4K the map renders at 4 pixels per point, and a marker held at 2 would take a quarter of the frame fraction it takes at 1080p. `ImmersiveMapVideoExportConfiguration.markerScale` is now stated against the reference canvas, so its default of 2 lands exactly on the map's own scale at every resolution and a 1080p export is unchanged. Nothing in an export tells the renderer how big a point is, and 1 sized a 1080p frame as if it were a 1920 by 1080 point canvas, which would now have produced hairline labels. The scale comes from the frame's short side against a 540 point canvas, so 1080p composes at 2 and 4K at 4: a 4K export is a sharper 1080p export rather than a wider one, and a 9:16 frame gets the same label density as a landscape one. Still capture already took `pixelsPerPoint` and is unchanged.

- The prepared-tile disk format is version 74 and the text revision is 7. A record of an older version is structurally valid but its geometry now means something else, and nothing in the payload encodes its unit, so the version is what keeps a warm cache from rendering half-size labels; the first launch after upgrading re-prepares tiles.

- `Tools/VisualReview` on iPhone is a guided pass that produces a report, so a visual review can be made by somebody who has never seen the repository. The phone build now opens on a start screen, renders the catalogue, walks straight into the pictures one at a time and ends on a summary with one button: **Make the report** packs the pass into a zip (`report.json` with the outcome, note, fingerprint and render path per scenario plus the device model, OS, Metal device name and commit; a plain-text `README.txt`; the verdict file unchanged so it drops back into the checkout; and `Renders/` with every picture judged), and **Send the report** hands that single file to the share sheet. The Mac build gets the same report from a toolbar item. Reports live in `Tools/VisualReview/Reports/` (gitignored) or the app's Documents container on a phone, and only the newest is kept.

- `Tools/VisualReview` renders per-scenario canvases. A scenario carries an `output` (pixel size plus scale), so the catalogue can hold phone scenarios: two new ones render Manhattan streets and San Francisco settlements at 1206x2622 with a scale of 3, the densest display and smallest canvas the engine targets, which is where undersized type shows up first.

### Removed

- The swift-protobuf dependency. The package now has no dependencies at all: tiles were already read by the hand-written zero-copy decoder in `Tile/Parse/Mvt/`, and the only thing still borrowed from the generated schema code was the property value type, which is now the internal `MvtValue` enum (one case per value kind the Mapbox Vector Tile specification defines, plus `.absent` for an empty `Value` message) next to `MvtGeometryType`. The generated `vector_tile.pb.swift`, the `.proto` schema and the `Package.resolved` pins went with it; tests build tile bytes through a small test-side encoder instead of the library. Nothing in the public API changes, and `ImmersiveMapFeatureProperties` reads exactly as before. What an app gains is measured on the iOS demo's Release archive, same commit before and after: the binary shrinks from 4.8 MB to 3.4 MB (over a quarter of it was the protobuf runtime, which the `Message` conformance pulled in whole even though the engine read seven optional fields) and the app bundle from 7.6 MB to 6.5 MB, the `SwiftProtobuf_SwiftProtobuf.bundle` resource bundle disappears from the app, a clean build no longer spends twenty-odd seconds compiling the library, and `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` no longer has an Apache 2.0 notice to carry. The README's app-size table is re-measured.

- **Breaking:** the tile-provider abstraction is gone; `.tileURLTemplate(_:headers:)` is the one way to point the map at an endpoint. Removed: the `ImmersiveMapTileProvider` protocol, `AnyImmersiveMapTileProvider`, `VectorTileProvider`, `ImmersiveMapTilesProvider`, `ImmersiveMapTileSource`, the `.tileProvider(_:)` view and settings modifiers, and the `ImmersiveMapSettings.tileProvider` field. What the provider actually did moved to where it belongs: the source (template, headers, TileJSON, base URL) lives in `TileSettings.NetworkSettings`; the label profile moved onto `VectorTileMapStyle`; the zoom coverage is `tiles.coverage.maximumZoomLevel`; the attribution default is the hosted credit, overridden with `attributionSettings(ImmersiveMapSettings.AttributionSettings(attributionOverride:))` when an app serves its own data; and cache identity is derived from the source fields and the style fingerprint. `ImmersiveMapOfflineController` is now created from a settings value (`init(settings:)`, defaulting to the hosted source) instead of a provider.

- **Breaking:** the API-key and authorization machinery is gone; the URL template and custom request headers are the one way credentials travel. Removed: the `.apiKey(_:)` view modifier and `ImmersiveMapSettings.apiKey(_:)`, the `AuthorizationMode` type and the `authorizationToken`/`authorizationMode` network settings. A key for the hosted service now attaches as a request header, `.tileURLTemplate("https://immersivemap.dev/tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}.mvt", headers: ["Authorization": "Bearer im_…"])`; a query-string credential belongs in the template itself. The loader no longer appends token query items or a bearer header of its own, and the prepared-tile cache identity no longer hashes an authorization mode. This is a major-version break: code using the removed symbols stops compiling and migrates to the template, the headers form, and `VectorTileMapStyle`.

- **Breaking.** The Mapbox provider and style are gone: `MapboxTileProvider`, `MapboxMapStyle` and `MapboxDefaultMapStyleConfiguration` (with its nested `LabelAppearance`, `LabelStyles`, `LayerStyles`, `RoadLayerStyles`, `RailwayLayerStyles` and `FeatureStyles`, the `.mapboxDefault` preset and the `labels(_:)`/`layers(_:)`/`features(_:)` builders) no longer exist, along with the Mapbox Streets label profile behind them, the `Examples/macOS/ImmersiveMapMapboxMac` example, its `IMMERSIVE_MAP_MAPBOX_ACCESS_TOKEN` scheme variable and the `Documentation/docs/mapbox.md` guide. The engine ships one first-party source, `ImmersiveMapTilesProvider` with `ImmersiveMapTilesMapStyle`, and every other MVT endpoint plugs in through `VectorTileProvider` with an `ImmersiveMapVectorTileStyle` of its own.

  An app that rendered Mapbox tiles can keep doing so through the generic path, which needs the values the deleted types used to hold: the tile base URL is `https://api.mapbox.com/v4/<tilesetID>` (the old default tileset was `mapbox.mapbox-streets-v8,mapbox.mapbox-terrain-v2`), the token travels as `.accessTokenQuery(parameterName: "access_token")` on the `ImmersiveMapTileSource`, the maximum tile zoom was 20, the style has to be written against the Mapbox Streets layer names, and the attribution Mapbox's terms require (`© Mapbox © OpenStreetMap` plus an "Improve this map" link to [mapbox.com/about/maps](https://www.mapbox.com/about/maps/)) must be declared on the provider, since the engine no longer knows to ask for it.

  Two things do not survive that move. Label placement falls back to `GenericVectorTileLabelProviderProfile`, tuned by the public `ImmersiveMapVectorTileLabelProfile`: it has no zoom gates, no rank boosts for capitals or population and no collision ordering between POIs and settlements, so a migrated map draws denser, flatter-ranked labels than the built-in Mapbox profile did, and the richer `VectorTileLabelProviderProfile` hook is internal. The parser's synthetic low-zoom ocean and sea labels also styled themselves under the Mapbox Streets `natural_label` layer, which only `MapboxDefaultMapStyle` answered, so they now render only for a custom style that handles that layer name; the first-party OpenMapTiles style labels oceans from the tiles' own `water_name` layer as before, and its rendering is unchanged.

  Because this removes shipped public API it is a breaking change; per the versioning note at the top of this file, the project is still pre-1.0 and does not promise semantic versioning yet.

### Fixed

- Roads are clipped against carriageway surfaces in the right coordinate space. The clipper received the surface rings flipped to render space while the road lines were still in raw tile space, so every road was cut against a mirror image of the area: lane paint survived inside real junction areas, piling the dashes of every entering street across the crossing, and roads at the mirrored spot were phantom-clipped. The regression test's fixture now sits off-centre, because the original one mirrored onto itself, which is how this shipped.

- The paint of a street is cut only by reconstructed crossings, not by every carriageway surface. With the clip fixed, a hand-mapped `area:highway` covering a whole street would have erased the street's markings end to end; the ribbons and kerbs are still cut by every surface, but the lane paint runs through a hand-mapped carriageway and stops only at a crossing.

- A street crossed by a chain of small crossings keeps its paint between them. An end created by cutting the street against a crossing's surface already stands at the edge of the gap; backing off by the half-carriageway inset as well ate every short piece twice over, and a street with frequent side-street junctions lost its markings for hundreds of metres.

- A junction area's kerb follows the junction. The carriageway surfaces the tiles ship from z15 (`subclass=junction_area`) reach the kerb pass in render space, where the y axis has already been flipped, and the line tessellator flips it again on the way in: every kerb was drawn mirrored about its tile's mid-line. What that painted was a thin dark outline lying wherever the mirror image happened to land, across a park, a block of buildings or a courtyard, with nothing around the junction it belongs to, and it appeared as the map crossed into z15 because that is where the source starts shipping the areas. The rings are converted back to tile space before tessellation, and a regression test pins every kerb vertex inside the area it belongs to.

- Lane markings are drawn from what the tiles state instead of from what a class suggests. The schema carries `lanes` and `oneway` and nothing about paint, but the markings were derived from a per-class default lane count whenever `lanes` was missing, and every two-way drive road got a centre divider: the map painted a centre line down residential back streets that have none, and lane lines inside roads whose lane count was a guess. Paint is now drawn only where the tiles state a lane count of two or more, and only for the through hierarchy (`motorway`, `trunk`, `primary`, `secondary`, `tertiary`); `minor`, `service`, tracks and footways draw as bare asphalt. Across the Moscow-centre tiles this takes the painted features from 79 to 37, and the ones left are the roads that actually carry paint.

- The paint on a carriageway breaks at junctions and nowhere else. A junction is where two named streets of class `minor` or above meet: a seam between two pieces of one street (which the stitcher leaves apart when their lane count or oneway differs) is not one, and neither is a service driveway or a parking aisle meeting a street. Reading every shared point as a junction lit a twelve-metre solid stretch at each of them, which turned a uniform broken line into dashes of wildly different lengths along a single avenue.

- Markings stop at junctions instead of running across them. The paint's half-carriageway inset applied at the two ends of a line, but a tiler merges a street into one line whose junctions are interior vertices, so a centre line ran straight over every crossing street, and a side street's paint painted over the avenue it met. A marking pass is now cut at every interior point another carriageway touches, and each piece is inset from its own new ends, so the paint stops short of the crossing on both sides. Footways and rails are not junctions for this purpose: counting them would break the line at every kerb ramp.

- Label languages are read under both field spellings a tile source can carry. The engine resolved a language only through the OpenMapTiles convention (`name_en`, `name_ru`, ...), so a source that passes OSM tags through unchanged (`name:en`, `name:ru`, ...) never matched and every label fell back to the native `name`, semicolon-joined multi-values included: an English-configured map labelled the continents "América" and "Afrika;أفريقيا". The fallback chain now tries `name_xx` and `name:xx` for each language before falling back, which is what the labels documentation already described. The low-zoom ocean fallback labels strip either spelling to the same language code, and the prepared-tile format version is bumped so cached tiles prepared with the old resolution re-prepare instead of keeping their baked native-name text.

- Non-flat roofs are shaped by the building instead of the tile grid. The first real `roof:shape` data showed the old roof path placing every ridge along whichever tile axis the footprint's axis-aligned box was longer in, and sampling a height field only at the footprint's own ring vertices, so a gabled rectangle had no gable at all (all four corners at the eaves made a flat lid below the wall tops), a pyramid had no apex, and anything rotated against the tile grid (most of Moscow) rendered as a creased sheet with edges cutting through the walls. The roof frame is now a property of the footprint itself: the ridge follows the long axis of the footprint's minimum-area oriented bounding box, invariant under rotation of the tile grid, and the newly parsed `roof:orientation` (`along`/`across`) and `roof:direction` (degrees or compass points, the downslope azimuth) override it where OSM says so. The mesh now contains the vertices each shape needs: gabled roofs split the footprint along the ridge line and the walls gain matching vertices there, so a gable end closes as a vertical triangle up to the ridge; hipped roofs (and `mansard`) inset the ridge and keep level eaves; pyramids and cones raise an apex over the area centroid; domes (and `onion`, `half-dome`) build latitude bands to a smooth cap; skillion roofs stay one plane but slope with the footprint or the tagged direction; `gambrel` renders as gabled. Everything is watertight between the eaves and the ridge or apex. A footprint the builder cannot shape honestly (a courtyard hole under anything but a skillion plane, a degenerate ring) falls back to the flat lid at the full height, since a wrong roof reads worse than a flat one. A roof is also built from the whole footprint the tile carries, into the tile source's buffer, and only then clipped to the tile square, so a building on a tile edge gets one continuous ridge instead of two halves framed separately that never meet; a footprint the tiler itself already cut (deeper than the buffer) is flattened rather than given the ridge of its visible piece. Tagged directions map through the parser's y-flip correctly, so a roof facing north faces north on screen. New visual review scenario `buildings.roofs.kremlin` keeps the shapes looked at where the data has all of them in one frame.

- The built-in camera control panel no longer sends the camera the wrong way around a bearing cap. Pressing rotate at the cap edge normalized the overshot angle across the forbidden arc and clamped it to the opposite edge, and the bearing follow then eased along the shortest path, which runs behind the compass: every step was clamped back to the cap, the follow stalled, and the panel's readout showed one edge while the camera sat at the other. The panel now saturates at the cap in the direction of travel, and the follow eases along the in-window path (through north) whenever the cap in force is narrower than the half turn, so the far edge is reached instead of stalled at. Before `bearingLimit(_:)` this was reachable only transiently on a globe mid-transition; the cap made it a stable configuration.

## [0.6.1] - 2026-08-13

### Fixed

- An app using the package no longer crashes at launch in the iOS Simulator. The cascade shadow maps rasterize through layered rendering (one pass writing three slices, with the caster vertex stages routing instances by `[[render_target_array_index]]`), and the simulator does not support it: a render pass asking for more than one slice fails Metal validation, which aborts the process rather than returning an error. Clearing the shadow fallback texture encoded exactly such a pass while the shared render resources were being built, so the crash landed before the first frame. Where layered rendering is missing, the fallback texture is now cleared one slice per pass and the frame renders without shadows, with a one-time console warning naming the reason so the difference from a device build is not mistaken for a rendering bug. Real hardware is unaffected: Apple5 and later covers every iOS 18 device, and mac2 covers every Mac.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-08-12

### Added

- Offline regions: download a geographic area once and the map renders it with no network connection. `ImmersiveMapOfflineController` is a standalone main-actor controller (no map view involved) that downloads an `ImmersiveMapOfflineRegion` (a rectangle plus a zoom span, antimeridian-crossing supported) into a pinned store under Application Support, with bounded-concurrency fetching over the same URL construction and authorization the live map uses, per-tile retry, resume that never refetches what is on disk, known-empty markers for tiles the source 404s (so coastal regions complete), an abort on authorization failure surfaced as `isBlockedByAuthorization` on the region status (so an app can say "fix the key" instead of offering a retry that would stop the same way), observable per-region progress (`regions` + `onRegionsChanged`), cancel, reference-counted removal (tiles shared by overlapping regions survive until the last one is deleted), and a `maximumTileCountPerDownload` guard. Serving needs no wiring: the store is namespaced by the provider's tile-source identity (the same hash that keys the prepared-tile cache), so any `ImmersiveMapView` with the same provider finds the regions on its own. `ImmersiveMapSettings.TileSettings.OfflineSettings.Mode` (via `.offlineTileMode(_:)`) picks how: `.automatic` (default) answers any failed tile request from the downloaded regions, `.offlineOnly` renders exclusively from them and never touches the network (including TileJSON discovery), `.disabled` ignores them. Offline bytes flow through the regular parse and prepared-cache pipeline, so a downloaded region warms the same caches the live map uses. New example `Examples/macOS/ImmersiveMapOfflineMac` downloads preset cities or a box around the current map center with live progress, cancel/resume/delete, and the mode switch; the Diagnostics section of `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac` gained the mode picker. `GeoCoordinate` is now `Codable`.

- `Posts/CrowdBloom`: the avatar-marker post. Fifty avatar markers stand over half a kilometre of Barcelona's Eixample, and the storyboard crosses sixteen zoom levels to show what the collision layout does with them: one flower cluster over the city at zoom 12.6, five flowers over the neighbourhood at 15.0, and fifty separate portraits at street level, with cones back to the doorways of the few that still have to give way, then the whole thing in reverse on the way out. Portraits are drawn on the CPU rather than fetched, so a render never waits on the network and every take is identical, and the crowd is laid out from the collision thresholds themselves (a flower forms within 0.35 marker widths and needs five members) instead of being sprinkled. Because avatar geometry is measured in drawable pixels while the map scales with the frame, every pixel-valued avatar setting is derived from the height of the frame being rendered, so the bloom lands on the same zoom, and the same second, in the preview window and in each export format.

### Fixed

- The prepared-tile cache can no longer serve corrupt geometry from its MTLIO fast path (format v31). An MTLIO load reports "complete" for bit-rotted, truncated, and torn files alike, and v30 had moved the geometry bytes out of the checksummed metadata envelope, so any such container rendered wrong triangles indefinitely. File-transport entries now store the blob's checksum (and the container's encoding) in the metadata, verified after every load, which also binds each `.ptile` to the exact `.ptgeo` content it was saved with: a crash between the pair's two writes, or a second engine sharing the cache namespace, produces a detected mismatch that removes the pair and re-parses instead of a silent mismatch that draws garbage. Decode validation is hardened the same way: a forged arena size is capped at 64 MiB instead of driving gigabyte allocations that were kept as "transient" and retried forever, a forged span table near `Int.max` fails into removal instead of trapping on arithmetic overflow (which crash-looped the app on every visit to the tile, since removal only ran after the throw), and the span table is checked against the arena schema derived from the same entry (span count, per-slot stride, index-width placement). The loader no longer deletes a healthy disk pair when materializing an ETag-matched entry fails transiently under memory pressure; removal is owned by one gated path and fires only for genuinely unreadable entries. The MTLIO capability decision has one owner: if the IO command queue cannot be created, the session degrades to inline entries instead of writing file entries no hit can load. And `preparedDiskCompressionEnabled` now reaches the geometry blob on the MTLIO transport: disabling it writes raw MTLIO-loadable files (recorded per entry, both variants stay readable) instead of being silently ignored for exactly the bytes the setting is about. The arena traversal itself is now defined once, as `TileArenaSchema`'s slot sequence: the plan writer, the factory's span reader, and the codec's validator all iterate it, so the two hand-mirrored traversals that could silently drift apart are gone, and the prepared-tile model lives in the `Tile` domain next to its codec and version constant.

- A fast trackpad zoom no longer stutters while it coasts to a stop. A scroll gesture has two halves: while the fingers are down its events carry `phase`, and after they lift the system keeps sending decelerating events carrying `momentumPhase`, which move the camera exactly the same way. Interaction rendering ended at the lift, so the whole momentum half was drawn with the render loop back on demand, a single requested frame at a time on a display link paused and unpaused for each one. A slow zoom hid this because it carries almost no momentum, so the switch happened on an already still map; a fast flick puts most of the movement there. Measured on a 50 Hz display over 300 frames with the same amount of camera movement, the p90 gap between frames was 1.43 refreshes for a slow zoom, 2.00 for a fast one, and 1.43 again once the momentum half counted as interaction.

- Tile loading no longer stops working in a process that builds several map engines over its lifetime. Each engine created its own `MTLIOCommandQueue`, every queue spawns IO threads and holds kernel-side state for as long as it lives, and past some number of them the IOGPU driver stops servicing submissions on freshly created queues entirely: loads park in the driver and their completion handlers never run, so cached tiles never reach the screen and nothing reports an error. An app that recreates its renderer (a settings change, a view torn down and rebuilt, a batch of still captures or video exports) walked into this; the queue is now created once per Metal device and shared.

- Shadows no longer pop in and out at the frustum edge while the camera pans. The cascade shadow maps rasterized casters only from the visible tile set, so a building standing just off-screen between the frame and the sun cast nothing into the frame, and panning made its shadow appear and vanish with the tile. Tile coverage now also resolves a sun-ward strip of off-screen tiles (the visible coverage polygon swept along the sun ray by the middle-cascade caster-height cap): those tiles are demanded at the tail of the priority order and rendered into the cascade maps only, never into the world pass, labels, or the backdrop. On the globe and with shadows disabled the strip is empty and nothing changes.

### Performance

- Saving a prepared tile no longer runs its LZFSE pass on the shared cache IO queue, and no longer holds the uncompressed arena in the queue: the container is compressed and staged before the save is enqueued, and the serial queue (which cold disk reads wait behind) only renames the staged file and writes the metadata. Container writes still run one at a time on a queue of their own, keeping the only write pattern the IOGPU driver was ever exercised with. The arena layout plan is also computed once per network load and shared by the GPU materialize and the disk save, instead of running the span measure walk and the 16-bit index narrowing twice over the same parse.

- The prepared-tile disk cache stores each tile's GPU bytes as one arena image instead of ~70 per-array fields (format v30). At save time the geometry is laid out exactly as the tile's single backing buffer will be laid out (a shared plan owns the span order, the 256-byte alignment, and the 16-bit index narrowing, so the cached blob and a freshly parsed arena are the same bytes); metadata (label placement inputs, run styles, road-label structures, the span table) stays a compressed property list. A cache hit therefore skips per-array decoding, index narrowing, and the measure pass entirely: the blob is copied into a fresh buffer in one memcpy and the buffer views are rebuilt from the span table. On Metal 3 devices outside the simulator the blob additionally lives in a sibling MTLIO compression container (LZFSE chunks) and a hit loads it straight into the tile's buffer through `MTLIOCommandQueue` with hardware-path decompression, so the bulk bytes of a warm tile never pass through a CPU decode at all; Intel Macs and the simulator keep the inline blob with the single memcpy. Span tables are untrusted input: the codec validates the offset chain structurally and the factory pins every span to its slot's element stride, so a corrupt entry fails into removal and a clean re-parse instead of a malformed draw. The transports use separate cache namespaces, and an unreadable blob (including a lost sibling file) removes the pair and re-parses.

- The render loop is driven by `CAMetalDisplayLink` instead of `CADisplayLink` plus a mid-encode `nextDrawable()`. The link is built from the host view's `CAMetalLayer` on both platforms and hands each tick's drawable together with the tick, so a frame starts with its drawable already in hand: the encode never blocks waiting for one, the system paces wakeups against actual drawable availability (which is what makes ProMotion pacing exact), and the frame is measured against the texture it really renders into instead of the layer's nominal size, closing a live-resize race. Camera and scene animations now sample the update's `targetPresentationTimestamp`, the moment the frame is predicted to appear on the display, rather than the callback's "now", which removes a frame of animation lag. The on-demand contract is unchanged: an idle map keeps a paused link and renders nothing at all. The platform `DisplayLinkFactory` seam (`CADisplayLink(target:selector:)` on iOS, `NSView.displayLink(target:selector:)` on macOS) is gone; the offline video export path never used the display link and is untouched.

- Translucently composited buildings no longer cost a dedicated offscreen pass on Apple GPUs. The composited path (translucent mode and the `solidAtHighZoom` transition) used to render buildings into an offscreen MSAA texture, resolve it, and sample that resolve back in the world pass, which on a TBDR GPU means flushing tile memory to DRAM and reading it straight back. The building image is now a second color attachment of the world pass itself: memoryless (it is cleared going in and discarded going out, so it never exists in DRAM), written by the building draw with the drawable attachment masked off, and read back per sample by the fullscreen composite through framebuffer fetch (`[[color(1)]]`), which also writes far-plane depth (`[[depth(any)]]`) to restore the pre-building depth mid-pass, keeping the documented "scene models ignore composited building depth" semantics exactly. One render pass instead of two, no intermediate texture allocation, no DRAM round trip. Intel Macs and the simulator keep the two-pass path; the only visual difference on Apple GPUs is that the composite blends per MSAA sample rather than over the resolved average, which can shift pixels where a building edge crosses a ground-color edge by at most the blend of the two.

- Every tile is backed by one Metal allocation instead of up to ~70: `TileBufferArena` sub-allocates all geometry layers and label runs from a single buffer (256-byte-aligned spans, exact measured length), the drawers bind sub-ranges by offset, and the memory cache accounts the tile at the backing buffer's real `allocatedSize`, so the page tail per buffer and the per-tile allocation churn disappear and the purgeable transitions touch one resource. On unified memory the parse thread writes straight into the final GPU allocation.

- All three shadow cascades render in one layered pass: the shadow map is a depth16Unorm texture array with one square slice per cascade, every caster draw carries `instanceCount = cascadeCount`, and the vertex stages route each instance to its cascade's slice via `[[render_target_array_index]]`, so building and model geometry is encoded once per frame instead of once per cascade. Layered rendering is supported by every GPU the package targets (Apple5 and later covers all iOS 18 hardware, mac2 covers every Mac). Receivers sample the array by cascade index and each cascade owns its full slice UV, which also retires the atlas seam insets and non-square texel handling.

- Warm-cache tiles that nothing references (neither the demanded set, nor the pinned world cover, nor a retained placement) park their buffers as purgeable-volatile once the in-flight frame window has passed, so the OS can reclaim them under memory pressure system-wide before jetsam looks at the process. A reclaimed tile is detected on its next lookup, drops out as a plain cache miss, and reloads through the normal demand path; a reused tile is pinned back untouched.

- Transparent space: `.transparentSpace()` (backed by `ImmersiveMapSettings.SceneSettings.space.isTransparent`) leaves everything outside the globe unpainted, so the app's own background, a color, a gradient or an image, continues behind and around the planet. A globe is not always a scene in orbit; it is often one element on a screen that already has a background, and painting space a flat color could only ever match a flat background exactly, with anything else showing as a square around the globe. The mode clears the frame to a fully transparent premultiplied pixel, skips the starfield layer as a whole (the space background, the stars and the visible Sun all draw there), and marks the map's `CAMetalLayer` non-opaque so the window server composites the drawable over what is behind it; on the way to the flat presentation the clear color still ramps to the opaque map color through the morph, so the plane arrives fully painted. This also removes the reason to clip the map view to a circle: with nothing painted outside the sphere there is nothing to clip, and a marker or avatar can hang off the limb the way a sticker hangs off a ball. On macOS the enclosing window still has to be transparent for the desktop to show through; the setting applies live, without recreating the renderer. Alpha handling was made correct across the renderer for it: every blended pipeline now accumulates coverage with `.one` on the alpha channel instead of `.sourceAlpha` (which squared it, so a 50% label, route or avatar landed at 25% over a transparent background, and translucent style layers left the globe surface itself slightly see-through), and FXAA carries the frame alpha through instead of forcing it to 1. On an opaque destination none of this changes a pixel.

- Tappable 3D scene models: `.onSceneModelTap { event in }` delivers a tap that lands on a model, and models now take part in `ImmersiveMapSelectionController` as `ImmersiveMapSelection.Kind.sceneModel` alongside avatars. Until now the only touchable things on the map were flat overlays (avatar markers and SwiftUI markers), so anything that was actually geometry in the world pass, an aircraft flying a route or a landmark placed at a coordinate, received nothing and a tap on it fell through to the background. Hit-testing reuses what the frame already computed rather than modelling the map a second time: `SceneModelAnchorMath` evaluates the sphere-to-plane morph once per anchor and bakes it into a model matrix, and the vertex shader is a plain `projectionView * modelMatrix * position`, so inverting those same two matrices gives a picking ray that is exact by construction. Hit areas therefore follow the model through flat mode, the globe, the morph between them, and any animation, with no per-model bookkeeping in the app and no second source of truth to drift. The ray is tested against the asset's bounding box *in the model's own space*, which makes it an oriented box rather than a bounding sphere: a diagonal aircraft keeps a hit area the shape of the aircraft instead of a ball of mostly empty air. Each frame publishes the volumes of the models it actually drew, built inside the same loop that emits the draw items, so frustum culling and the globe horizon gate cull the hit volumes exactly as they cull the drawing: what is on screen is what can be tapped, and a model beyond the horizon or removed from the controller stops being tappable the moment it stops being drawn. The snapshot travels the same path as the avatar one (published on GPU completion, monotonic frame-index guard, reset on renderer recreation), so a late frame from a discarded engine can never resurrect a stale layout. Overlapping models resolve nearest-to-camera; avatar markers, being overlays drawn over the world pass, take the tap ahead of a model behind them, and one nothing listens to falls through to the model underneath. A model whose whole on-screen footprint is smaller than a 44 pt touch target grows to one around its center so distant models stay reachable by finger, while a model large enough to aim at keeps its own outline, which means the fallback can only fill a gap and never steal a tap from geometry that was actually hit. `ImmersiveMapSceneModelTapEvent` carries the descriptor, the tap point, and the coordinate the model was *drawn* at: mid-flight that is a point on the path, while the descriptor already holds the destination. Known limits: hit-testing uses the bounding box rather than the triangles, so a tap in the empty corner of a concave model's box counts as a hit; and depth is not consulted, so a model hidden behind a solid building is still tappable. Selection carries no appearance for models: the engine draws the asset as authored and highlighting stays the app's to do. See `Documentation/docs/scene-models.md`.

- Routes on the globe, 3D models that fly along them, and a camera that travels with them. `ImmersiveMapGeoPath` describes a trajectory as waypoints joined by great-circle arcs plus an altitude profile (`base + peak * sin(pi * fraction)`, so a flight path arcs away from the planet and back), parameterized by arc length rather than by waypoint index. The same value feeds both new pieces, which is what guarantees the model rides exactly on the line that is drawn. `ImmersiveMapRoutesController` and the `.routes(_:)` modifier draw a route as a ribbon in the map world pass. The far half of an arc disappears behind the globe because the shader tests each centerline point against the sphere itself, not against the depth buffer: depth is only ever as complete as the surface that wrote it, and the globe surface is a chord mesh at the limb, absent under the polar caps, and missing entirely until tiles arrive, so a route that trusted it painted the far side of the planet over the near one along the rim. The test is the distance from the planet's center to the segment eye-to-point, evaluated per fragment: the point is hidden exactly when that segment passes within the radius, and only when the closest approach falls inside the segment, which is what separates "the planet is between us" from "the planet is behind the point". A plane through the tangent circle would be cheaper, and is exact for points lying on the sphere, but the shadow a sphere casts is a cone rather than a half space: an arc lifted over the far side leaves the cone while still sitting behind that plane, and a plane test cut it short. Because the test runs on the point where it actually is, altitude included, a lifted arc carries past the limb on its own while a ground track stops at it, and no term of it depends on the camera's angle, so tilting and turning are covered by construction. A model in front of the line still covers it, through depth as before. The width is given in points and is expanded in the vertex shader, so it stays constant on screen at any zoom and through globe rotation, with edges antialiased by the same signed-distance convention the text and marker shaders use. `progress` truncates the ribbon at an exact arc-length fraction (not at the nearest tessellated point), and `setProgress(id:_:duration:)` eases it for a line that draws itself. On the scene model side, `animate(id:along:duration:curve:appliesHeading:appliesPitch:completion:)` moves an existing model along the path over a real duration, with the course taken from the trajectory tangent and the pitch from the altitude gradient, so a plane banks into the climb and noses down on descent; `completion` fires exactly once on the main thread, `true` on arrival and `false` when the animation is superseded, cancelled through `cancelPathAnimation(id:)`, or dropped because the model was removed, the view went away, or a settings change recreated the renderer, so journey chains never hang. While a path animation runs it owns the model's coordinate, altitude, heading and pitch; competing `move` / `setAltitude` / `setOrientation` calls for that id are ignored for its duration, everything else including `setScale` still applies, and cancelling leaves the model where the flight got to, with that position written back into the descriptor so a later mutation starts from where the model actually is. `ImmersiveMapCameraController.follow(path:duration:curve:options:completion:)` travels the camera along the same path: it resolves its own point of the path from the shared metrics and curve, so a model and the camera started with the same duration stay in step without anything passing between the render side and the camera. By default the camera keeps its zoom and pitch, so an app can keep zooming while it travels, and turns to the course, closing half the remaining gap to the point every `smoothingHalfLife` so a curved path does not feel rigid; anything the user does to the camera cancels it, exactly as it does a flight. Routes also take a dash pattern, measured along the line as it appears on screen rather than along the geometry, so dashes keep their size while zooming instead of stretching with the world. Internally the sphere-to-plane morph is now evaluated once, on the CPU, by a shared `GeoSurfaceFrameMath` extracted from the scene model anchor path, so the ribbon centerline and the model anchor cannot drift apart; the route shader only widens the centerline. Known limits in this version: routes draw in globe presentation only (including the whole morph, fading out over its last tenth) and are absent on the fully flat map, the model animation is not restricted that way; no gradient along the line; routes are excluded from tour video export; a route with a zero altitude profile can stipple against the surface, so give ground tracks a small `baseAltitudeMeters`; where a route runs out at the limb the ribbon's last half width can reach a couple of pixels past the silhouette, since the width is a screen-space expansion of a centerline that is gated in world space; and the camera's course mode is subject to the engine's own globe bearing limit, so on a zoomed-out globe the turn is clamped rather than fought for. See `Documentation/docs/routes.md`.

- The globe paints its own surface before any tile arrives. Every visible tile slot that no content covers yet is drawn as a blank tile in the map background color (`SceneSettings.mapClearColor`, white by default) under the tile atlas, so a globe whose tiles are still loading, or one with a hole in coverage, reads as a blank planet rather than a shell with space showing through it. A blank slot is lit by the same day/night shading, limb glow and horizon haze as the tile that replaces it, so the arriving tile changes the texture and nothing else, and it writes the depth that tile would have written, which gives the world pass something to occlude with from the first frame. Each fill draws the exact slot geometry its tile will draw, and that is what makes the swap invisible: a first version painted the whole sphere in one coarser draw beneath the tiles, and a coarse mesh touches the true sphere at its own grid vertices while the finer tile mesh chords slightly under it there, so every such vertex poked through the ocean as a background-colored dot. The polar caps keep their own style-derived fallback color.

- Building depth cues and shadow polish. Three related changes make building masses read as separate volumes without reintroducing an analytic lighting model. (1) Two subtle tonal terms in the building shader separate faces that flat shading merged into one mass: a hemisphere term keeps roofs at the base color while walls darken slightly (the sun-facing side a touch lighter than the far side, so the edge between two walls is always visible), and a vertical gradient darkens walls toward the ground, the classic ambient-occlusion cue that grounds buildings. Both multiply the base color, fade out with the shadow factor (a shadowed face keeps the pure shadow color instead of stacking darkening on darkening), and leave the shading contract (flat color plus shadows) unchanged. (2) Geometrically self-shadowed walls now darken to only 35% of the shadow strength instead of the full cast-shadow darkness: a wall turned away from the sun is lit by the sky, not occluded like a ground spot under a roof. (3) Shadow edges no longer crawl along facades during fast camera movement: the cascade windows are anchored at the camera's look-at point, so panning drives buildings through them, and the previously hard sharp-to-coarse texel switch at a window boundary swept across walls as a visible seam. Cascade transitions now cross-fade over an eight-texel band.

- Attribution badge customization. `ImmersiveMapSettings.AttributionSettings` gained size presets (`.small` / `.regular` / `.large`, where fonts, paddings, corner radius and the maximum badge width scale coherently), six safe-area-aware positions (four corners plus `.bottomCenter` / `.topCenter`; leading/trailing now follow the layout direction, so RTL apps get the mirrored corner), and an optional `textColor` (RGBA `SIMD4<Float>`, the copyright line renders at 76% of the given alpha). The partial modifier `.attributionSettings(isVisible:size:position:textColor:isProvidedExternally:)` restyles the badge without replacing the whole settings value, and everything applies live without recreating the renderer. A map that starts with a hidden or empty badge logs a one-time-per-process console warning (`os.Logger`, subsystem `ImmersiveMap`, category `Attribution`) reminding that map data licenses require visible attribution near the map; an app that shows the credit itself declares that with the new `.attributionProvidedExternally()` modifier, which silences the warning and changes nothing else.

- `ATTRIBUTION.md` in the repository root (moved out of `Documentation/docs/`, next to `LICENSE`) now covers the license side, not only the badge API: the exact credit each built-in provider requires, why it has to sit on or next to the map (an About or Settings page alone does not satisfy the license), why the obligation does not depend on zoom level or on how much of the map is shown, a split of what the engine guarantees versus what stays with the app, a worked example of drawing the credit in your own SwiftUI layout, and links to the primary sources (ODbL, the OSMF attribution guidelines, OpenMapTiles, Mapbox). The hidden-attribution console warning and the `.attributionProvidedExternally()` documentation now link to it, so the warning points at the explanation instead of leaving the reader to search the repository.

- Map view reuse (on by default). When a SwiftUI screen with an `ImmersiveMapView` goes away, its platform view (renderer, GPU tile cache, atlas pages, Metal layer, gesture wiring) is parked for a short time (30 s by default) instead of being destroyed, and the next `ImmersiveMapView` adopts it warm, so switching between map screens skips the first-frame rebuild entirely. New settings are reconciled on adoption through the regular settings-apply planner, so adopting with a different configuration is safe (a changed tile provider still recreates the renderer, now cheaply on top of the shared resources). An adopted view keeps its previous camera unless the new view provides an explicit camera position or an attached camera controller. The parked view is released on time-to-live expiry and on memory warnings. Note that SwiftUI creates the incoming screen's view before dismantling the outgoing one, so the first transition between two map screens still builds fresh; every later transition adopts. Opt out with `.viewReuse(false)` or tune via `ImmersiveMapSettings.ViewReuseSettings`. Together with disk-first tile loading and the shared render resources below, this completes the "warm caches, cold view" work: returning to a map screen is now instant.

- Process-wide shared render resources. Immutable GPU state is now created once per process and reused by every map view and every settings-driven renderer recreation: the Metal shader library, all ~26 render and compute pipeline states, depth-stencil states, the MSDF text atlases with their glyph tables (previously two PNG decodes + GPU uploads and two JSON metric decodes per view), the POI sprite atlas (SF Symbol rasterization), the avatar marker SDF, and the procedural sphere and polar-cap geometry. Creating a second `ImmersiveMapView`, or changing settings that rebuild the renderer, no longer pays any of that cost; per view remain only the command queue, dynamic buffers, tile/label caches and the small config-baked pieces (starfield colors and star buffer, polar-cap palette, avatar style values). This is the second half of the "warm caches, cold view" fix started by disk-first tile loading below.

- Disk-first tile loading. A tile with a prepared entry on disk now renders immediately from that entry while the network request runs in the background as pure revalidation: a matching ETag confirms the content already on screen (no re-parse, no re-upload), a changed ETag re-parses the fresh bytes and swaps the tile in place, and a failed download simply leaves the served content up instead of arming retry backoff. Previously the loader always waited for the network stage before consulting the prepared cache. With `Cache-Control: must-revalidate` tiles, that put a network round-trip in front of every tile even when the disk held a byte-identical parsed copy, which is exactly the "warm caches, cold view" delay on app launch and on every newly created map view. The mechanics are guarded against the failure modes optimistic rendering invites: a served tile stays **requestable** in the render store until some load confirms or replaces it, so a revalidation lost to `cancelAll` (memory warning) or a parse failure of fresh bytes retries on later frames under the normal backoff instead of pinning stale content for the session; the network slot is released when the download completes, not when the (serialized) disk decode does, so cold tiles never queue behind warm ones; prepared-entry decoding moved off the shared serial cache queue so concurrent loads decode in parallel; and a parse failure no longer deletes the disk entry that is currently displayed. Entries saved from responses without an ETag cannot be revalidated and are re-parsed as before.

- A lower zoom bound: `ImmersiveMapSettings.CameraSettings.minimumZoom` and the `.zoomRange(minimum:maximum:)` modifier. The camera previously clamped only from above, with the bottom pinned to zoom 0. Both bounds are now enforced in one place, so gestures, zoom commands and camera flights all obey them, including the overview arc of a long `fly(to:)`, which used to climb to a globe view regardless. A minimum above the globe-to-flat transition keeps the map flat for good; an omitted bound is left as configured, and an inverted range collapses to `maximumZoom` rather than trapping the camera.

- Directional shadows on the flat map: extruded buildings and 3D scene models cast real-time shadows onto the ground, onto neighboring buildings and onto each other, via three cascaded shadow maps rendered into one 3:1 depth atlas in a single depth-only pass. All cascades are fitted to **pose-invariant discs** around the camera's look-at point with radii in multiples of the camera distance (1 / 3 / coverage), so tilting or rotating the camera changes nothing about shadow sharpness or coverage. Cascades are sampled with Castaño's 3x3 tent PCF (four hardware-bilinear compares with computed weights): a crisp ~2-texel edge that is orientation-independent, with no staircase on shadow boundaries diagonal to the shadow grid (the far edge cast by a roof almost always is), about a meter of transition at street zooms in the near cascade; the middle cascade (3 camera distances) keeps meter-scale texels where most visible shadows land at a tilted camera, and the far cascade (16 by default) shares the filter at its coarser texel inside the fade zone. Every comparison uses the receiver-plane depth predicted from screen-space derivatives (Isidoro receiver-plane bias), so flat roofs and the ground need almost no constant bias: no acne striping, and the small per-frame bias (derived from the actual texel footprint) keeps shadows attached to building bases. The texel grid is stabilized against camera motion: window sizes quantize in √2 steps (capping density overshoot at 1.41× while integer-zoom re-normalization still lands on exact ×2) and window centers snap to whole texels in pan-anchored coordinates (computed in Double, because at high zoom the pan offset is world-sized and Float ULP exceeds a texel), so shadow edges do not crawl during movement and stay glued to the map across integer-zoom re-normalization. Shadows are on by default and tunable through `.shadowSettings(_:)` / `.shadows(isEnabled:)` (`ImmersiveMapSettings.ShadowSettings`: strength, per-cascade map resolution, far coverage in camera distances); the shared light direction that drives both shading and shadows moved into settings as `.sceneLight(direction:)` (default unchanged: light from the south-west, shadows fall north-east). All shadow settings apply live without recreating the renderer. Known v1 limits: the ground shadow under a translucent building is not attenuated by the composite alpha; buildings clipped at tile boundaries have no boundary walls, so edge-on light can leak slightly (softened by two-sided caster rasterization); the texel grid re-anchors once when crossing the antimeridian and when a quantized window size doubles (rare, zoom-dependent); sharpness steps down once at the near-cascade border (~2 camera distances out); the coarse horizon backdrop receives no shadows by design and hides the coverage edge in the horizon fog.

- API keys: the `.apiKey(_:)` view modifier (backed by `ImmersiveMapSettings.apiKey(_:)`) authorizes tile requests against the hosted service. The key travels as an `Authorization: Bearer` header rather than a query parameter, so byte-identical tiles share one CDN cache entry across customers. It applies to whichever provider is configured and may be written before or after `tileProvider`; `ImmersiveMapTilesProvider(apiKey:)` now sends its key the same way (previously `?key=`).

- 3D scene models: `ImmersiveMapSceneModelsController` plus the `.sceneModels(_:)` modifier anchor USDZ/OBJ models at geographic coordinates. Models render inside the world pass with depth, MSAA, and the building light, in flat mode, on the globe, and through the morph between them: the anchor rides the same unfurl wave as the surface, the tangent frame tilts from the sphere normal to flat up, and the meter scale blends from true globe scale to the Web-Mercator `1/cos(latitude)` convention, so a model stays glued to the map at any zoom. Descriptors carry altitude in meters, heading/pitch/roll, a meter `scale` multiplier, and `fitDiameterMeters` to size arbitrary assets; `move` animates along a great circle with distance-derived duration, `setOrientation`/`setScale`/`setAltitude` ease with shortest-arc quaternion interpolation. Assets load asynchronously via Model I/O (`MDLAsset` → `MTKMesh`, no new package dependencies), deduplicate by source URL, retry with cooldown, and live in a memory-pressure-aware LRU cache; rendering stays on-demand throughout. Models are excluded from tour video export. See `Documentation/docs/scene-models.md`.

- `ImmersiveMapTourVideoRecorder`: exports a camera tour to a QuickTime video file. Attach it with `.tourVideoRecorder(_:)` on `ImmersiveMapView` and call `export(shots:establish:configuration:to:)` with the same `ImmersiveMapCameraTourShot` list a live tour uses. The tour is rendered offline into a second, headless render engine at an exact fixed timestep: every frame waits for its tiles (with a configurable timeout), a pre-roll settles label and avatar fades before the first frame, and the on-screen map stays interactive throughout. Avatar markers render from a detached copy of the avatar state taken at export start (renderer snapshots are consumed destructively, so two engines cannot share one controller), and SwiftUI markers are rasterized once and composited onto each frame at their projected positions with the live overlay's anchor and horizon-fade semantics; both can be excluded via `includesAvatars` / `includesMarkers`, and `markerScale` controls marker rasterization density. Output defaults to HEVC 1920×1080 at 60 fps in `.mov` with BT.709 tagging; codec (`.hevc`/`.h264`), resolution, frame rate, and bit rate are configurable via `ImmersiveMapVideoExportConfiguration`. Progress is reported through `onProgress`, `cancel()` deletes the partial file. The attribution badge is host-view chrome and is not part of the exported frames. See `Documentation/docs/tour-video-export.md`.

### Fixed

- The debug HUD's `dt`/`fps` figures are real again: the panel used to print seconds-since-renderer-creation as a millisecond delta and derive fps as 1000 divided by it, so `dt` grew without bound and fps decayed toward zero over a session. The diagnostics now carry the actual frame delta.

- Dark palettes no longer grow a bright blue spot over the north pole and a dark hole over Antarctica. Mercator tiles stop at the maximum latitude, and the caps that fill the rest continue the last row of tiles at their rim but fade into a single color at the pole itself. That color was taken from `baseColors.water` in the north and from `baseColors.tileBackground` in the south, which only works for a light style: a dark one that sets the two backgrounds and forgets the water keeps the daylight blue at the north pole, and its near-black tile background bears no relation to the white Antarctica the southern rim actually shows. The southern pole color is now a palette color of its own, `ImmersiveMapSettings.StyleSettings.BaseColors.polarIce`, defaulting to the white the daylight palette has always drawn, so a style declares its ice instead of having it inferred from a background. The `night` and `blueprint` palettes of the settings example now set `water` and `polarIce` alongside the backgrounds.

- Attribution now credits the data source instead of the engine. The badge previously showed "Immersive map, © 2025-2026 ImmersiveMap contributors" over OpenStreetMap data, which satisfies neither ODbL nor the OpenMapTiles and Mapbox terms. Attribution is now a property of the tile provider (`ImmersiveMapTileProvider.attribution`): the built-in tiles credit OpenStreetMap, OpenFreeMap and OpenMapTiles, `MapboxTileProvider` credits Mapbox and OpenStreetMap, and a provider that declares no attribution renders no badge at all instead of borrowing someone else's name.

### Changed

- Every example and post scheme runs its app in `Release` rather than `Debug`, with `ONLY_ACTIVE_ARCH = YES` in that configuration so a run builds the native slice instead of a universal binary. These projects exist to be watched, and a debug build of the engine drops frames on the very scenes they are built to show. The iOS example, which had no shared scheme at all, got one. Debugging one of them means switching its Run action back to `Debug` by hand.

- The eight macOS example projects moved from `Examples/` into `Examples/macOS/`, so the single iOS host is no longer buried in a flat list of nine. Each moved project keeps its scheme name, targets and sources; only its `XCLocalSwiftPackageReference` changed, from `relativePath = ../..` to `relativePath = ../../..`, and `ImmersiveMap.xcworkspace` now groups them under a `macOS` node inside `Examples`. `Examples/ImmersiveMapIOS` stays where it was, and the documentation links under `Documentation/docs/` follow the new paths.

- Examples are organized by what the reader copies, not by feature count. `ImmersiveMapLabelsMac`, `ImmersiveMapEarthSceneMac` and `ImmersiveMapBuildingsMac` were three projects around one pattern: take `ImmersiveMapSettings.default`, change a field, hand it back. They are now three sections of a single `Examples/ImmersiveMapSettingsMac`, which keeps one `ImmersiveMapSettings` in state and passes it whole to `ImmersiveMapView(settings:)`, with a sidebar switching between its branches. The move paid for three areas that had a documentation page but no running example: **Style** (day, night and blueprint palettes for the built-in provider, plus attribution badge size and position), **Presentation** (the globe-to-flat window, the globe radius and the zoom range, with the resolved transition printed next to the camera zoom and latitude) and **Diagnostics** (the debug HUD, FXAA, the frame loop and both tile caches). Because the whole settings value is in one place, the app can also show what an edit costs: it runs the engine's own `ImmersiveMapSettingsApplicationPlanner` on every change and names the result on screen (`liveApply` versus `rebuildPreparedData` and `recreateRenderer`), and the sliders bound to the expensive fields commit when the drag ends instead of on every value, which is the pattern an app should copy. `ImmersiveMapIOS` went back to being what the README's app-size measurement claims it is: the smallest app that renders a map, with the copy of the scene-model demo removed and the touch-only `.cameraControlZones()` left as the one thing it shows that macOS cannot. The rule going forward, recorded in CONTRIBUTING: a new project is for an integration scenario (a provider, a controller, views of your own), while a new field on `ImmersiveMapSettings` gets a section in `ImmersiveMapSettingsMac`.

- The default attribution text is now the single line "© OpenStreetMap © OpenMapTiles" (previously two lines: "© OpenStreetMap contributors" + "OpenFreeMap © OpenMapTiles"); the link still leads to the full license story at openstreetmap.org/copyright, and OpenFreeMap asks for no credit of its own. The `.openStreetMap` preset is one-line as well ("© OpenStreetMap contributors"). An `ImmersiveMapAttribution` with an empty `copyright` renders a one-line badge: no empty second row, no spacing.

- The camera control zones in the bottom corners (drag on the leading side to tilt, on the trailing side to zoom) are now **off by default** and opt in through `.cameraControlZones(pitch:zoom:)`. They were always on, invisible, and captured drags that would otherwise pan the map, so the first encounter reads as a panning bug rather than a feature. Pointer scroll zoom (trackpad, mouse wheel) moved out of the zoom zone into its own `ScrollZoomGesture` and keeps working either way.

- Building and scene-model shading dropped its analytic lighting model (previously Phong, briefly a soft lambert): faces keep their flat base color and darken **only** where the shadow map says the static sun is occluded. Walls turned away from the sun are shadowed by a geometric self-shadow test (a face with N·L ≤ 0 is in shadow by definition, so the map is neither needed nor trusted there, which also kills acne striping on walls nearly parallel to the sun rays), everything else darkens per the shadow map: one consistent shadow system, and neighboring buildings now visibly cast onto walls. `.sceneLight(direction:)` still defines the sun; it now drives shadow casting alone.
- `ImmersiveMapSettings.AttributionSettings` no longer carries `title`, `copyright` and `linkURL`. It keeps `isVisible` plus an optional `attributionOverride`, and the resolved text comes from the active tile provider unless the app overrides it. `VectorTileProvider` gained an `attribution` parameter (`.none` by default, with `.openStreetMap` provided for plain OSM sources). Switching tile providers now updates the badge live.
- README documents where the default tiles come from (an OpenMapTiles-schema planet assembled from OpenFreeMap data, i.e. OpenStreetMap data under ODbL), how to point the engine at your own MVT source, and how attribution is resolved. Status wording that described the package as unfinished was dropped in favour of a feature list.

### Performance

- Unit-range fragment math now runs in half precision across every remaining shader (MSDF text, tile fill with fog, building lighting cues, globe day/night shading and rim glow, FXAA luma, POI sprites, avatars with badges and beams, routes, scene models, the building-image composite), extending the measured starfield precedent (~15% for ALU-bound fragments on A-series GPUs, rate-identical on M-series, lower register pressure on both). Values that need range or gradient precision stay float: uv derivatives, texel-space SDF distances, dash arclengths, world positions, fog distances and all shadow projections. Output targets are 8-bit, so no rendered pixel can tell the difference.

- The shadow cascade atlas is `depth16Unorm` instead of `depth32Float`: half the memory (24 MB instead of 48 MB at the default resolution) and half the bandwidth of every shadow compare, up to 8 per fragment. The cascades are refit each frame to a tight caster range with depth clamping and the receiver bias is texel-derived, so 16 bits lose nothing visible; the offscreen shadow suites pass unchanged.

- Rendering now respects the device's power situation: serious thermal pressure caps the frame rate at 60, critical at 30, and both (as well as Low Power Mode) revoke ProMotion headroom, while Low Power Mode alone keeps the configured interaction floor. An idle map still renders nothing at all.

- ProMotion frame rates on macOS: the display link range now mirrors the iOS behavior instead of pinning to the configured rate, so 120 Hz MacBook Pro panels pan and animate at 120 during interactions while the label fade keeps its low cadence.

- The tile `URLSession` matches its per-host connection cap to `maxConcurrentFetches`, marks tile requests `assumesHTTP3Capable` so the first connection to an HTTP/3 CDN can start on QUIC (with transparent fallback), and drops the request timeout from 60 s to 30 s so a stalled tile releases its fetch slot sooner.

- ProMotion frame rates on iOS. During interaction and animations the display link is now offered a frame-rate range from the configured `interactionFramesPerSecond` (still the floor, 60 by default) up to 120 Hz, so ProMotion panels pan, zoom and animate at 120 instead of being pinned to 60; non-ProMotion hardware clamps to its own maximum and behaves exactly as before, and label fades keep their deliberately low cadence. On iPhone, rates above 60 additionally require the host app to declare `CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone` in its Info.plist; the macOS path is unchanged.

- Every fragment of the ground, roads, buildings and globe pays for shadow sampling only when shadows are actually on: `sampleShadowFactor` now exits before the cascade math when the bound shadow uniform is disabled (`strength <= 0`), instead of projecting all three cascades and evaluating six screen-space derivatives per pixel and only then returning 1.0. Shadows are gated off for whole frames routinely (the globe presentation, no casters on screen, a low sun), and the disabled uniform is bound to every world fragment in those frames.

- First-view startup compiles the ~29 render and compute pipeline states (and the other device-only shared resources: the text atlases, the avatar marker SDF, the polar-cap geometry) on all cores via `DispatchQueue.concurrentPerform` instead of strictly one after another on the main thread. The created set and the synchronous-before-first-frame contract are unchanged; the wall-clock cost of the most expensive startup stage divides by the core count.

- Tile geometry indices are written as `uint16` whenever a layer stays within 65535 vertices, which covers nearly every layer of every tile; oversized layers keep `uint32`, decided per buffer at creation. The same narrowing applies to the static globe surface grid and polar-cap grids. This halves index bandwidth and index memory in the GPU tile cache, and building geometry is drawn up to four times per frame (world pass or building image plus two shadow cascades), so the bandwidth saving multiplies. The prepared-tile disk format still stores 32-bit arrays and is not invalidated.

- The prepared-tile disk cache verifies integrity an order of magnitude faster: the envelope checksum hashes 8-byte words instead of single `Data` bytes on both encode and decode (envelope version 2; version-1 entries stay readable through the byte-wise path, so existing caches survive). Reads also map the file (`.mappedIfSafe`) instead of copying it into memory up front, so pages stream in as the checksum and the decompressor walk the payload.

- MVT polygon and line decoding reserves each ring's exact point capacity before the decode loop (the command integer announces the count, capped by the remaining geometry), removing repeated array growth from the hottest parsing loop.

## [0.4.1] - 2026-07-30

### Changed

- The macOS example app (`ImmersiveMapMac`) now ships the full demo scene: a looping cinematic tour (globe, tilted pass through the globe-to-flat morph, Tokyo streets, Dubai, back to orbit) built on `ImmersiveMapCameraTourController`, SwiftUI city-card markers, avatar markers with badges, and an enlarged GPU-ready tile cache via `.tileSettings(memoryCacheSizeInBytes:)`. Start the tour with the Cinematic Tour button or R, stop with R, Esc, or any map gesture.

### Fixed

- Avatar markers now project through the same shared projector as tiles and SwiftUI markers (`GeoScreenProjectionMath`): the unfurl-wave morph and the soft per-point horizon. Previously avatars used a plain sphere-to-plane lerp with a stale horizon threshold, so mid-morph at high tilt a far-away avatar (Moscow with the camera over Dubai) drifted across the screen, floated above the horizon and popped in and out while zooming. The duplicated projection math in `AvatarSelectionProjector` is gone.
- Markers far behind the globe horizon no longer flash across the viewport at the tail of the globe-to-flat morph. The unfurl wave carries such a point from the far side of the sphere to its flat position far off-screen, and for a few frames the trajectory crossed the visible area over empty ocean (tiles never render that far coverage mid-flight). During the morph a marker now stays hidden unless its sphere position is within the spherical horizon plus a margin for what the unfurl legitimately brings into view.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-07-30

### Added

- `ImmersiveMapCameraTourController`: runs the camera through a sequence of `ImmersiveMapCameraTourShot`s (position, flight options, hold time), chaining flights back to back. Supports an optional establishing jump, looping, a finish callback, and stops automatically when the user starts a gesture (without occupying the public `onUserInteractionBegan` callback). Useful for cinematic fly-throughs, demo reels, and onboarding tours.
- Declarative SwiftUI markers: `.markers(items, coordinate:anchor:content:)` on `ImmersiveMapView` anchors arbitrary SwiftUI views to geographic coordinates. Markers are repositioned every rendered frame (flat, globe, and through the morph, riding the unfurl wave), fade behind the globe horizon, and stay fully interactive: buttons and gestures inside marker content work while the map keeps gestures elsewhere. See `Documentation/docs/markers.md`.

### Fixed

- Toggling `enableCameraUIControls` no longer tears down and recreates the whole platform map view: the SwiftUI body keeps one stable view identity, so the renderer, tile caches and controller attachments survive UI chrome toggles.
- A dismantled host view no longer detaches camera, avatars and selection controllers from a newer host view that reuses them (SwiftUI creates the replacement representable before dismantling the old one). Detach is now ownership-checked, which unbreaks camera commands, including `ImmersiveMapCameraTourController` tours, across view recreation.
- Renderer recreation now completes an active camera flight with `success == false` instead of silently swallowing its completion, so chained `fly` calls cannot hang on a never-resumed continuation.
- SwiftUI markers on the far side of the globe no longer leak through the horizon mid-morph. The horizon threshold now relaxes per point with the unfurl-wave local phase instead of the global transition: a still-spherical point keeps the strict spherical test. Tiles hide this leak behind the depth test, the view overlay has none.
- The globe polar caps no longer flicker or break into gray triangle fans. The cap smears a single edge-texel row of the boundary tiles, but sampled it with automatic mip selection: near the pole the uv derivatives explode (meridians converge, the longitude wrap adds a derivative discontinuity), so the sampler dove into deep atlas mips where texels average unrelated tiles, and every atlas repack reshuffled the colors. The cap now samples the base mip explicitly and feathers toward the pole color, so narrow coastal features at the boundary row (like Ross Sea water) no longer smear radial needles across the cap.

### Changed

- `CameraFlightAltitudeStyle.overviewFirst` now flies a true van Wijk & Nuij overview arc: the camera climbs out (up to a full-globe view for intercontinental flights, proportionally lower for short hops), covers the distance at the apex, then dives to the target. Pan speed is coupled to altitude, so the camera crawls near the ground instead of streaking across street-level tiles. Previously the style merely delayed a direct zoom interpolation, so long flights panned the whole route at the start zoom.
- Renamed the avatar tap API: the `onMarkerTap` modifier is now `onAvatarTap`, and `ImmersiveMapMarkerTapEvent` is now `ImmersiveMapAvatarTapEvent` (same fields). No compatibility shims. The term "marker" now belongs to the SwiftUI marker overlay API introduced in this release. The avatars guide moved from `Documentation/docs/markers.md` to `Documentation/docs/avatars.md`.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-07-24

### Removed

- OpenStreetMap / Shortbread provider (`OpenStreetMapTileProvider` / `OpenStreetMapMapStyle`). The package now ships two providers: the built-in ImmersiveMap tiles and Mapbox. Custom MVT sources remain supported via `VectorTileProvider`.
- City night lights feature.

### Added

- `buildingExtrusionMode` modifier on `ImmersiveMapView` (and `StyleSettings.buildingExtrusionMode`): flat-mode extruded buildings can now render fully opaque with `.solid`, in addition to the default `.translucent` blending. `.solidAtHighZoom(startZoom:endZoom:)` blends translucent buildings into solid ones across a zoom range (default 17...18) as the camera zooms in. Switching the mode (or `buildingExtrusionAlpha`) applies live, without recreating the renderer.
- Reworked globe ↔ flat transition: the globe unrolls as a wave travelling from the view center outward, the morph geometry completes before the surface swap, and horizon culling relaxes continuously through the morph.
- Flat-mode horizon: the far coverage ring is filled with pinned backdrop-zoom tiles up to the horizon, with haze anchored to the vanishing line. The flat view distance now matches the globe side of the transition.
- Latitude-aware camera and tile LOD, and a mip-mapped tile atlas for steeply tilted views.
- POI visibility is derived from rank budgets instead of zoom ramps; iconless POI labels are gated by camera zoom; road labels are gated by clipped visible area and content density.
- `preparedDiskCompressionEnabled` in `.tileSettings(...)`: LZFSE compression of the prepared tile disk cache is now optional. Disabling it trades disk footprint for noticeably less CPU (and battery) while exploring uncached areas.
- Debug HUD: label collision boxes overlay and a total tiles counter.

### Changed

- The package now builds in Swift 6 language mode with strict concurrency.
- Tile loading is split into independent network and parsing stages: a network slot frees right after the download, and parsing runs in separate tasks bounded by the device's core count. Slow networks no longer starve parsing, and long parses no longer block new downloads.
- Default in-memory tile cache budget lowered from 512 to 256 MiB; evicted tiles reload cheaply from the prepared disk cache. The previous budget is still available via `.tileSettings(memoryCacheSizeInBytes:)`.

### Performance

- Globe atlas pages (hundreds of MiB of textures) are released after the map stays in flat presentation for a few seconds, instead of being retained until a memory warning.
- Empty tile geometry layers no longer allocate placeholder GPU buffers (a tile without bridges or tunnels used to allocate buffers for every road phase).
- MSDF text atlases load into private GPU storage via a staging blit, dropping their shadow CPU copies.
- Label rendering skips redundant Metal bindings (font atlas, styles, shifts), and label compute work is batched into two encoders per frame instead of a pair of encoders per road tile record, with frame constants bound once per pass.
- The flat far ring is handed fully to the z3 backdrop with a steeper distance LOD, and building extrusion resolution reuses precomputed footprint bounds and areas.

### Fixed

- Flickering light seams on extruded buildings: thin background-colored lines along facade junctions that shimmered with camera movement. Buildings are now always drawn opaque with plain depth testing - solid mode directly in the world pass, translucent mode into an offscreen building image that is composited over the map with `buildingExtrusionAlpha` - replacing the single-sample "winner ID" discard that clashed with MSAA. Translucency is now uniform across the whole building silhouette, buildings correctly occlude each other, and building geometry is rendered once per frame instead of twice (per-feature building color alpha is no longer factored in).
- Fog is applied to the morph surface at its morphed positions instead of the sphere's, removing fog popping during the globe ↔ flat transition.
- The flat horizon no longer jumps between zoom bands: the far plane is pushed out and horizon haze saturates before the coverage edge.
- Line-only boundary styles no longer fill their polygon features.
- Debug tile overlay watermarks stay flat and bounded at high tilt.
