# Tile pipeline

The flat map's coverage is ring rules (`FlatRingRuleCoverage`, `FlatRingRules`, edited in the debug panel): each rule is a zoom drop and a distance in tiles of the target zoom, counted on the grid from the look-at tile (the target tile under the point the camera looks at, ring 0 that tile alone). The ground inside a rule's square of rings and outside the previous rule's is one band drawn at the target zoom minus the drop (never as coarse as the pinned z0 world cover, which the flat map neither draws as a backdrop nor uses as a stand-in: there is no z0 fallback on the plane, beyond the last rule the haze paints the horizon over the clear colour). A band's tiles are every tile of its zoom that the frustum footprint meets inside the band, so nothing outside the view is placed, and a coarse tile is placed for the part of it that is in view. The look-at point does not move with a turn or a tilt of the camera and the squares run along the grid, so neither changes a tile's rule, and a pan moves the squares by whole tiles. Overlaps are resolved by the tile-priority stencil, and nothing is placed beyond the last rule's square. The default is four rules: the exact tiles to ring 1, one level coarser to ring 2, two levels coarser to ring 3, and four levels coarser to ring 20 (both without lines). A rule is rasterizable (`FlatRingRule.rasterized`; a picture is always 256 texels a side, the one entry of `FlatRingRules.rasterResolutions`, because a larger picture per pictured tile is memory the far ground cannot have), which is a permission and not a place: where the ground turns from geometry into pictures is the raster zone (`RasterZone` in `Tile/Raster`, edited in the debug panel), a distance from the camera to the ground point in camera distances, so it follows what is on screen and knows nothing of the tile grid. The ground nearer than the zone's start is always vector. Past it a rasterizable tile's picture fades in per pixel (the alpha is computed in `TileRaster.metal` from the pixel's distance) and is the whole ground past the fade. A picture is one layer in the middle of its tile's paint order: `FlatMapSurfaceRenderSubsystem` sorts each tile against the zone (`RasterZone.tileDraw`: geometry alone, blended, picture alone) and draws the ground in three steps, under the pictures the pictured families (`GroundLayerGroups`: the plain fills always, the building footprints and the ground lines by their switches) of the tiles the zone's edge crosses, then the pictures, then over them everything that is geometry at every distance, the families a picture leaves out and the roads of every tile, since a road is never part of a picture. Each picture is rendered once by `TileRasterizer` (the flat ground drawer over the one tile through a straight-down camera, into a mipmapped texture, in its own command buffer ahead of the frame's) and kept by `TileRasterStore` for as long as frames ask for them, then drawn by `TileRasterDrawer` as one textured quad per tile: a picture that is the whole ground writes the rank band under the ground owner state, a blended one writes nothing and sits between the last fill rank and the first ribbon. A stand-in for a tile still loading stays vector. The HUD's camera lines show each rule's zoom, last ring and tile count, and its raster resolution where it has one. The sphere reads the same rules (`GlobeTileCoverage`): its quadtree walk keeps the frustum and horizon rejects, which stand where the plane has its footprint, and takes each tile's zoom from the rings between the look-at tile and the tile on the grid (`GlobeRingMath`), the columns closing at the antimeridian and the rows ending at the poles. Ground past the last rule is asked for at the pinned z0 cover, since the sphere has no haze to paint it. The sphere takes every switch of a rule: the zoom drops, the distances, the line switch (its ribbons pass skips the sources of lineless bands) and the pictures. The raster zone works on the globe as on the plane, by the same distance in camera distances (the camera looks at the sphere's front point, which is the render origin): `GlobeVectorSurfaceRenderSubsystem` sorts a rasterizable tile by its bounding sphere (every tile blended through the unfurl, where the bound does not hold) and draws the plane's three steps fitted to a sphere with no ownership prepass, first every vector source finest first in one call so the stencil is marked, then the pictures, then the lines pictured tiles keep as geometry. A picture on the sphere is the plane's own texture (`TileRasterPictures`, one rasterizer and one store for both grounds, so the surface switch at the end of the unroll re-renders nothing) laid over a grid built from the vertex id in `TileSphereRaster.metal`, as many cells a side as `GroundGeometrySubdivider` splits a tile of that zoom, through the vector ground's projection (pure and morph states in `TileSphereRasterPipeline`, the morph with the unroll's clip distance). It holds every fill, sits at one rank between the last fill and the first ribbon, and writes depth and the stencil under the sphere's owner state when it is its tile's whole ground, because the horizon reads ground off written depth. The rules come in sets by target zoom (`RingRuleSets`, edited in the debug panel): each set owns the zooms from its first to the next set's and carries its own tuning beside its rules (`RingRuleSetTuning`: the raster zone, the roads' thinness fade, the buildings' footprint areas, read through `DebugOverlayControlState.snapshot(forTargetZoom:)`), a frame reads the set its target zoom falls in, and the default is two sets, the globe's zooms up to 6 (`FlatRingRules.globeDefault`, stepping down fast and dropping lines early) and the plane's from 7. Per-frame demand starts in `TileWorkingSetSubsystem` → `TileRenderStore` (`TileWorkingSetStore`: exactly the frame's demanded tiles plus the pinned z0 world cover, released the moment demand stops naming them) → misses go to `ImmersiveMapNeedsTile` (`Tile/Loading`): a disk stage first (a prepared tile on disk answers the load with no network request and no revalidation; freshness is the prepared cache's TTL and identity namespace), then bounded-concurrency async/await download and parse with dedup FIFO, retry/backoff, and two disk caches (raw and prepared). Parsing/tessellation: `TileMvtParser` + the `Mvt` target (`MvtTileDecoder`/`MvtGeometryDecoder`/`MvtAttributeDecoder`) + clippers + the internal `Earcut` port → `PreparedTileCPU` → `MetalTileFactory` → GPU `TileBuffers`, where one Metal allocation per tile sub-allocates every geometry layer and label run. Completion invalidates a frame with `.tileAvailable`. Every slot-holding stage (disk, network, parse) has a deadline (`ImmersiveMapNeedsTile.StageDeadlines`): a load whose stage outlives it is retired by the loader's watchdog (the task cancelled, the lane slot freed, a `stalled in <stage>` failure recorded for the HUD and the retry backoff, and the retry wake armed so the on-demand loop re-requests the tile without a gesture), so no answer that never comes (a disk read, an MTLIO completion, a server) can hold a slot or hide a hole for the rest of the process. The debug HUD's tile list is refreshed from `TileLoadingStatusReporter` by the panel's own timer between frames (`DebugOverlayHUDSnapshotStore.attachTileLoadingStatus`), because a frame publishes a snapshot only through a 0.2 s throttle and the loop then sleeps: without the refresh the last tile to land stayed in its previous stage on screen for as long as the camera stood still, which read as a hung load. Rows show their stage age once it passes two seconds. The streetscape (the measured carriageway surfaces and road paint) is data a tile carries or does not: the parser merges every layer whose features the schema reading finds to be roads into the first of them (the decoder's `MvtDecodedTile.append(_:to:data:)` re-bases the tags; the `Mvt` target knows no layer name), derives per road layer whether it carries reconstructed surfaces or measured paint, and tells the style (`ImmersiveMapFeatureStyleContext.layerCarriesStreetscape`); the built-in style draws carriageways with their paint where it does and a street map's strokes where it does not. There is no streetscape setting and no second request. Downloaded offline regions (`Tile/Offline`, driven by `ImmersiveMapOfflineController`) are a third local source feeding the same pipeline: `TileSettings.OfflineSettings.Mode` decides whether they answer only failed network requests (`.automatic`), everything (`.offlineOnly`), or nothing (`.disabled`). Unlike the two caches, which live in Caches, the region store lives in Application Support and is namespaced by the tile source identity.
